\chapter{Results of milestone J1}\label{ch:t2-resultats}

\begin{origine}
\textbf{Where we start}: a program built by five lots, each proved on its own by its control case, and twelve criteria declared on 15 October 2026 before the first line of code (\fichier{decisions/ARCHITECTURE.md}, § 7). Volume~I had predicted what such a program could do; it had never seen it run.

\section{What is measured, and how}\label{t2res-sec:protocole}

\paragraph{The model and the task.} OpenAI's gpt-oss-120b in its native MXFP4 format: a GGUF file of \num{63387346208} bytes, 36 layers, 128 experts per layer of which 4 serve each token, \SI{13.25}{\Mo} per expert; 4\,608 experts in all, \SI{61.07}{\Go}, and \SI{1.69}{\Go} of weights read at every token (attention, routers, output head) \mes~(\fichier{mesures/gpt-oss.md}, § 1). Its development twin, gpt-oss-20b (\num{12109566624} bytes, 24 layers, 32 experts), is already larger than the graphics card. The speed task has been fixed since Volume~I: the 708-token wikitext prompt (SHA-256 \texttt{eadbe87e}…), then 256 greedy tokens, in mode A0 (no expert cache in RAM: every miss is read from disk).

\paragraph{The decision rule.} It is Volume~I's, declared before any measurement (study 06, § 7.5) and taken over unchanged by the architecture (§ 7.2).

\begin{definition}[Noise, real difference]\label{t2res-def:reel}
Let a configuration be measured by two passes $X_1, X_2$ of one palindrome. Its \emph{noise} is $\beta = |X_1 - X_2| / \bar X$; when a configuration has several pairs, its noise is the largest noise of a pair. A configuration $B$ differs \emph{really} from a configuration $A$ if
\[
\Bigl|\frac{\bar X_B}{\bar X_A} - 1\Bigr| \;>\; 2\,\max(\beta_A, \beta_B) + 3\,\%.
\]
\end{definition}

The noise of a program against itself is the yardstick: a difference smaller than twice a program's distance to itself is not a difference. The 3\,\% margin protects against a series too short to see its own noise. The trial wrote it as ``twice the noise and 3\,\%''; the verdicts of the round apply it in the additive form above, which is stricter. No verdict of this chapter changes from one reading to the other \calc~(\fichier{memoire/outils/tome2\_resultats.py}).

\paragraph{A second path for every number.} The numbers of this chapter are not copied from the reports: a committed tool (\fichier{memoire/outils/tome2\_resultats.py}) rereads the passes themselves, recomputes means, noises, ratios, intervals and floors, compares them with the verdicts the lots published, and stops with an error at the first discrepancy. It found one, which was not a mistake of the lots but an ambiguity of reading (the cost of the guard, \cref{t2res-sec:garde}).

\section{Exactness: coded equals raw, to the bit, over 5\,120 steps}\label{t2res-sec:exactitude}

\paragraph{What is compared.} Lot F writes two files of the same model: the \emph{coded} \texttt{.trc}, whose every expert is compressed losslessly by the rANS coder with per-lane tables, and the \emph{raw} \texttt{.trc}, with the same expert bytes copied as they are. The engine reads the first by decoding on the card, the second by copying. If the coding is truly lossless and the engine deterministic, both must return the same logits, to the bit; the smallest difference would be a fault.

\begin{proposition}[C3 by construction; the cache does not enter the computation]\label{t2res-prop:c3}
If the coded and raw files give, for every expert, a VRAM slot equal byte for byte, the engine returns the same logits to the bit on every token sequence, whatever the number of slots, the cache policy and the arrival order of reads.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The products of an expert read its slot and the activation $h_2$. When they read, the slot holds the decoded bytes of that expert: the order of the card's three streams guarantees it (lemma~4 of lot O, \fichier{moteur/O-pour-le-memoire.md} § 6.1), and those bytes are equal in both files by hypothesis (C1 for the coded file, a copy for the raw one, C2 for the transport). The cache only chooses \emph{where} an expert sits, never \emph{which bytes} it holds; the weighted sum of the $k$ experts runs in the router's order $i = 0, \dots, k-1$, not in the slots' order. The other inputs (resident weights, KV cache) do not depend on the expert file. Every kernel being a deterministic function of the bytes it reads (no floating-point atomic addition, fixed-tree reductions), the logits are equal.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{How to compare \num{5120} distributions without keeping them.} An output distribution has \num{201088} f32 logits: keeping them for the 10 windows of 512 positions would take $\num{5120} \times \num{201088} \times 4 = \SI{4.12}{\Go}$ \calc. The engine keeps instead the 64-bit digest (prefix of SHA-256) of the logits of each step, \num{40960} bytes in all; two steps equal to the bit have the same digest, two different steps a different one except for a collision, of probability $2^{-64}$ per pair. A second path, independent of the digest, compares the \texttt{.kld} files written by both passes byte for byte.

\section{Speed}\label{t2res-sec:vitesse}

\subsection{The serial floor (C5)}\label{t2res-sec:c5}

\paragraph{The formula.} A token served \emph{without any overlap} would pay every stage of its path one after the other. Its time is then the sum
\[
t_{\text{serial}} = t_{\text{disk}} + t_{\text{PCIe}} + t_{\text{dec}} + t_{\text{GEMV}} + T_0,
\]
with $t_{\text{disk}}$ the chunk bytes read per token (engine counter) divided by the throughput of a disk-only pass in the same session, $t_{\text{PCIe}}$ and $t_{\text{dec}}$ the card times of the copies and decodes per token (the engine's CUDA events), $t_{\text{GEMV}} = (A + R)/\SI{235}{\Go\per\second}$ with $A$ the resident weights read and $R = k \cdot L$ expert slots, and $T_0 = \SI{3.5}{\milli\second}$ Volume~I's fixed cost. C5 requires at least $0.8 / t_{\text{serial}}$ tokens per second; $0.8 = 1 - 2 \times 10\,\%$, the noise of a 4\,MiB read at queue depth 4 measured in Volume~I.

\paragraph{The calculation, step by step} (session O4, pass 1, \mes~for every term):
\begin{align*}
t_{\text{disk}} &= \frac{\SI{0.7073}{\Go}}{\SI{1.848}{\Go\per\second}} = \SI{382.7}{\milli\second}, &
t_{\text{PCIe}} &= 59 \text{ misses} \times \SI{1.28}{\milli\second} = \SI{75.8}{\milli\second}, \\
t_{\text{dec}} &= 59 \times \SI{0.92}{\milli\second} = \SI{54.2}{\milli\second}, &
t_{\text{GEMV}} &= \frac{\SI{1.686}{\Go} + 144 \times \SI{13.25}{\Mo}}{\SI{235}{\Go\per\second}} = \SI{15.3}{\milli\second},
\end{align*}
hence $t_{\text{serial}} = 382.7 + 75.8 + 54.2 + 15.3 + 3.5 = \SI{531.5}{\milli\second}$, a floor of \num{1.881} token/s and a threshold of \num{1.505}. The engine measures \SI{457}{\milli\second} per token, \textbf{\num{2.186} tokens/s}: it goes \emph{under} the sum, because the copies and decodes of a layer overlap the reads of the following misses of the same layer. \Cref{t2res-fig:plancher} redoes this calculation for every pass of the round.

\paragraph{The physical efficiency (C9).}\label{t2res-sec:c9} The architecture (§\,7.2) declared a criterion published and not judged: the measured throughput divided by the ceiling computed at the measured $h$. Neither lot O nor the trial had computed it; the reprise of the judgement does it here, on the terms of the floor \calc~from \mes. The ceiling is Volume~I's token equation with no speculation and no remaining latency ($\mathcal E = 1$, $t_{\text{lat}} = 0$), every stage overlapped:
\[
t_{\text{ceiling}} = \max\big(t_{\text{disk}},\ t_{\text{PCIe}},\ t_{\text{dec}} + t_{\text{GEMV}}\big) + T_0 .
\]
Pass 1 of J1: $\max(382.7;\ 75.8;\ 54.2 + 15.3) + 3.5 = \SI{386.2}{\milli\second}$, a ceiling of \num{2.589} tokens/s; measured \SI{457.4}{\milli\second}, hence an efficiency of $386.2 / 457.4 = \mathbf{0.84}$ (pass 6: 0.84). Over the round's six f16-KV passes, from \num{0.64} (pass 02, the slowest) to \num{0.82} (pass 03). The engine thus holds 84\,\% of the throughput it would have if the link and the card hid entirely behind the disk; the missing 16\,\% are the $t_{\text{lat}}$ term of the equation, the latency that the disk's short queue lets show, the one prefetching aimed at (\cref{t2res-sec:j1plus}). Redone by \fichier{memoire/outils/tome2\_reprise.py} from \fichier{res-plancher.csv}.

\paragraph{What limits J1, and what Volume~I had predicted.} The disk reads on average \num{1.6} chunks per layer (59 misses over 36 layers): the queue seldom has its four requests in flight, and the engine draws only \SI{1.55}{\Go\per\second} of useful bytes from the disk where a disk-only pass at depth 4 draws \num{1.85} \mes. Volume~I had fitted an ``LRU'' locality scenario on a single published point; it predicted there, for a \SI{4.8}{\Go} VRAM cache, a hit rate $h_v = 0.590$ and, in mode A0, a speed marker of \num{2.15} tokens/s (the 326 slots of J1 make \SI{4.3}{\Go}). The engine counts $h = 0.591$ over its 326 slots and measures \num{2.18} \mes. The agreement is to be taken for what it is: a scenario fitted on one point, whose shape the 20b locality measurement had already contradicted, lands right at J1's operating point.

\paragraph{Why the number of threads changes everything.} llama.cpp maps the file into memory (\texttt{mmap}); an expert missing from the page cache is read through page faults, and a faulting thread waits for its page before continuing. With $n$ compute threads there are thus at most $n$ reads in flight. Little's law \cite{little1961} ties the throughput $X$ of a system to its mean number of requests in flight $N$ and their residence time $R$: $X = N/R$. As long as the disk is not saturated, $R$ varies little and throughput grows with $N$; Volume~I measured this growth of disk throughput with queue depth. From 6 to 16 threads the baseline gains \num{54}\,\%; from 12 to 16, nothing discernible: the plateau begins. This reading is a hypothesis consistent with the measurement, not a measurement of the queue depth itself.

\subsection{Stream prefill}\label{t2res-sec:flux}

\paragraph{The cost, posed.} In the step loop, a prompt of $P$ positions reads, layer by layer, the missing experts of every position:
\[
B_{\text{loop}} = \sum_{p=1}^{P}\sum_{\ell=1}^{L} (1 - h_{p,\ell}) \, k \, t,
\]
with $t \approx \SI{12.03}{\Mo}$ per chunk. By stream, each layer reads once every expert requested by at least one position: $B_{\text{stream}} = \sum_\ell |U_\ell \setminus V_\ell| \, t$, where $U_\ell$ is the set of chosen experts and $V_\ell$ those already in VRAM. Under uniform routing, $\E|U_\ell| = E\,(1 - (1 - k/E)^P)$; for $E = 128$, $k = 4$, $P = 708$: $(1 - 1/32)^{708} = e^{708 \ln 0.96875} = e^{-22.5}$, and $U_\ell$ is the whole set. Hence $B_{\text{stream}} \le 36 \times 128 \times \SI{12.03}{\Mo} = \SI{55.4}{\Go}$, against $B_{\text{loop}} \approx 708 \times 144 \times 0.55 \times \SI{12.03}{\Mo} \approx \SI{675}{\Go}$ \calc, a ratio of about 12.

\paragraph{Measured.} \SI{26}{\second} instead of \SI{7}{\minute}\,\SI{36}{\second} for the 708 tokens, $\times 17.5$, the ratio of the mean throughputs of passes \texttt{s2-11} to \texttt{s2-14} (\num{27.15} against \num{1.554} tokens/s; per pass, \numrange{26.9}{27.4} against \numrange{1.46}{1.65}) \mes. Lot O's note wrote \barre{7\,min\,50\,s}, a duration that does not come from these passes: on top of the bytes, the loop pays one synchronisation per layer and per position, which the stream pays once per layer. llama.cpp prefills at \numrange{3.1}{4.0} tokens/s: troncons does it seven times faster. Exactness holds by the same \cref{t2res-prop:c3} and by control F (\num{5120} digests equal to the loop's on the 20b; equal \texttt{.kld} files on the 120b in the trial).

\subsection{The cost of the integrity guard}\label{t2res-sec:garde}

\paragraph{Predicted.} The guard hashes every chunk read (\SI{0.71}{\Go} per token) on four threads, at \num{1.07} to \SI{1.10}{\Go\per\second} per thread \mes, in parallel with the copy and the decode; the step waits for all its hashes before returning its token. The work, $0.71 / 1.07 \approx \SI{0.65}{\second}$ of core time per step, is spread over four threads (\SI{0.17}{\second} of wall time) under a step of \SI{0.46}{\second}; only the hash of the last chunk stays on the critical path, at most
\[
\SI{12.25}{\Mo} / \SI{1.07}{\Go\per\second} \approx \SI{11.4}{\milli\second},
\]
i.e.\ \SI{2.5}{\percent} of a \SI{457}{\milli\second} step \calc. The round's declared prediction wrote 2.4\,\%, with 1.1\,GB/s and 460\,ms (\fichier{mesures/moteur/O/corrections/seuils-declares.md}); at the slowest measured throughput, 1.07\,GB/s, it is 2.5\,\%.

\paragraph{Measured.} The guard's wait is \SIrange{5.2}{5.4}{\milli\second} per token over the four guarded passes \mes. The declared ABBA series (passes 02 and 05 guarded, 03 and 04 unguarded) gives a gap of $-12.3\,\%$ for a threshold of \SI{26.8}{\percent}: \emph{not real}; the disk varied from \SIrange{1.51}{1.95}{\Go\per\second} from one pass to the next, and that is what the gap measures. At equal disk throughput (passes 05 and 04, \num{1.708} and \SI{1.715}{\Go\per\second}), the gap is $-1.6\,\%$ (published $-1.7\,\%$ on the rounded values \num{1.900} and \num{1.932}). The second-path tool first found $-4.3\,\%$ by counting the four guarded passes: passes 07 and 08 are guarded too, but belong to the f32-KV series; this is no fault of the lot, it is the number of another series. Opening costs \SI{0.95}{\second} more (the table of digests of the embedding rows: \SI{0.615}{\Go} read and hashed), and memory \SI{1.9}{\Mo}, the same for both models.

\subsection{What J1+ kept, killed, left open}\label{t2res-sec:j1plus}

\begin{description}
\item[Kept: the stream] ($\times 17.5$ at prefill, \cref{t2res-sec:flux}).
\item[Killed: prefetch by the next layer's router.] After the attention of $\ell$, the engine applies the norm and router of $\ell+1$ to the residual and reads the predicted chunks ahead. A right prediction removes a read from the critical path; a wrong one adds a read on a disk that already limits. With $p$ the share of predictions served and $f$ the share of a token's time that overlap can hide, the gain is about $f - \frac{1-p}{p}\,s_{\text{disk}}$; here $p = 0.692$, the bytes read rise by \num{34}\,\% (953 against 710\,MB per token) and $f \approx 80/460 = 0.17$: the loss dominates. Measured: $-11\,\%$ (noise \SI{1}{\percent}) \mes. The mechanism would come back with $p > 0.9$ or so, or a disk that does not limit.
\item[Open: 12\,MiB reads.] Reading every chunk as one request of \num{12582912} bytes moves the read into the fast regime of the disk measured by lot E: $+30\,\%$ in median (\num{2.18} $\to$ \num{2.84} tokens/s), every B pass above every A pass, but a noise of \SI{16.2}{\percent} between the two B passes (\cref{t2res-def:reel}; \barre{21\,\%}, the gap divided by the mean of A): the threshold is $2 \times 16.2 + 3 = \SI{35.4}{\percent}$ against $+\SI{30.4}{\percent}$, the rule is not crossed in four passes. Neither established nor refuted.
\item[One pass, to be confirmed: the warm cache.] \SI{1}{\Go} of warm cache divides stream prefill by \num{2.2} (\num{27.1} $\to$ \num{12.5} tokens/s) without giving anything to generation (\num{1.90}) \mes. Nobody had measured it; one pass does not make a series.
\end{description}

\section{Fidelity: how far from the exact mathematics of the model}\label{t2res-sec:fidelite}

\subsection{The instrument}

\paragraph{The divergence.} For a position, let $p$ be the next-token distribution of the truth (the model's forward pass in double precision, on the same weights) and $q$ that of an engine. We measure
\[
\mathrm{KL}(p \,\|\, q) = \sum_{i=1}^{V} p_i \,(\log p_i - \log q_i) \;\ge 0,
\]
the Kullback--Leibler divergence \cite{kullback1951}, in nats, over a vocabulary of $V = \num{201088}$ tokens, and the share of positions where the engine's top choice is the truth's, with its 95\,\% Wilson interval \cite{wilson1927}. For a small perturbation $\Delta z$ of the logits, $\mathrm{KL} \approx \tfrac12 \mathrm{Var}_p(\Delta z)$: the divergence is second order in the error.

\paragraph{Why against the truth, and not against llama.cpp.} Two correct engines diverge: on Qwen3.5-4B, by \num{0.0055} (Volume~I); one engine cannot be the judge of another. The architecture therefore posed C4 against the exact mathematics: troncons must be at least as close to them as llama.cpp, both measured by the same instrument.

\paragraph{The file format, and what it costs.} All engines write their logits in llama.cpp's \texttt{.kld} format: per position, $\log q$ quantized on 16 bits between a floor and the maximum, with a step of about $\num{2.44e-4}$ nat. Two consequences, proved in \cref{t2e-sec:numpy}: a computation identical to the truth departs from it by at most half a step per token above the floor; and the tokens under the floor receive excess mass, so that the KL of an exact computation is \emph{negative}, of the order of $-\num{4.8e-4}$. This bias is common to all files and sets the zero of the instrument.

\paragraph{Why f16 rounding suffices, posed.} f16 has 11 bits of significand: rounding to nearest gives $\tilde k = k(1 + \delta)$ with $|\delta| \le u_{16} = 2^{-11} \approx \num{4.9e-4}$ (the binary16 format of IEEE~754). For an attention score $s = c \sum_{j=1}^{64} q_j k_j$ ($c = 1/8$),
\[
|\tilde s - s| \;\le\; c\, u_{16} \sum_j |q_j k_j| \;+\; \gamma_{64}\, c \sum_j |q_j \tilde k_j|,
\qquad \gamma_{64} = \frac{64\, u_{32}}{1 - 64\, u_{32}} \approx \num{3.8e-6},
\]
to first order, the second term being the f32 dot product ($u_{32} = 2^{-24}$, \cite{higham2002}). The attention output inherits a relative error of the same order, through the softmax weights and through $\tilde v$. With an f32 cache, only the kernels' error remains, $u_{32}/u_{16} = 2^{-13} \approx 1/8\,192$ times smaller; \barre{the KL being second order, it would be divided by about $8\,192^2 \approx \num{6.7e7}$: from $\num{3.08e-3}$ towards $\num{5e-11}$, well under the step of the format. This order of magnitude assumes a linear error; routing flips are not linear, but they vanish with it.} This estimate of work package O is struck out by \cref{thm:t2r-amplification}: the no-switch term is divided by $8\,192^2 \approx \num{6.7e7}$, but the switch term, linear, only by \num{8192}; the KL in f32 is therefore at most of the order of $\num{3.08e-3}/\num{8192} \approx \num{3.8e-7}$\,nat \calc{} (\cref{sec:t2r-f32-theorie}), well under the step of the format ($\approx \num{2.4e-4}$\,nat). The measurement below remains the only judge. A router that picks 4 experts out of 128 turns a small continuous error into a discrete one when two scores are close: this is why the 120b, with 128 experts, flips more than the 20b, with 32.

Replayed by the cache simulator on the prompt's trace, this loss moves $h$ from \num{0.459} to \num{0.442} and misses from \num{77.9} to \num{80.4} per token: $+3.2\,\%$ of bytes read, the order of the speed cost when the disk limits \calc~on a measured trace. The generation measured in f32 (\num{1.50} against \num{2.13} tokens/s) \emph{is not} that cost: the text changes (\texttt{72304d85}…, which starts with ``...'' like the truth, then simulates tool calls like llama.cpp's greedy output), and the loop of the f16 text raised its cache hit rate to \num{0.590} against \num{0.435}. Comparing two speeds on two different texts measures the texts. The default stays f16 until the arbiter decides seam N; the round proposes f32.

\section{What the results say together}\label{t2res-sec:ensemble}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Exact}: the coded path equals the raw path to the bit, over \num{5120} steps and from one build to another; Volume~I's lossless coding costs the output nothing.
\item \textbf{Bounded}: \SI{384}{\Mo} for \SI{63.4}{\Go}, $+\SI{3.7}{\Mo}$ when the model takes \SI{51}{\Go} more; llama.cpp holds \num{28.5} times more memory (\num{29.7} times in C6's configuration).
\item \textbf{Faster than the baseline}: \num{2.22} times the best baseline measured, guard included; seven times at prefill.
\item \textbf{Correct}: closer to the truth than llama.cpp on both models; and, with an f32 KV cache, equal to the truth up to the format.
\item \textbf{Limited by the disk}: \SIrange{68}{72}{\percent} of the serial floor; the next gain is in the disk queue (longer reads, or more reads in flight) and in the disk itself (\cref{ch:t2-discussion}).
\end{enumerate}

\begin{faux}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{C6 crosses two sessions}: troncons and the swept baseline did not run in the same palindrome; the ratio carries a between-session noise the rule does not measure. The same session, in the trial, gave $\times 2.89$ against a weaker baseline.
\item \textbf{The baseline may be better still}: neither more than 16 threads, nor \texttt{--mlock}, nor other values of \texttt{--n-cpu-moe} were swept. The $\times 2.22$ is a ratio to the best baseline \emph{measured}.
\item \textbf{C4 on the 120b rests on one window} of 255 positions and on the prompt; one window is not ten. The numpy truth and lot R's reference read the same GGUF and the same reading of the model: a common convention error would pass (\cref{t2e-sec:faux}).
\item \textbf{``f32 KV = truth''} is measured on one window of 512 tokens and one prompt of 708 (120b), one window of the 20b; not on the ten windows.
\item \textbf{The cost of the guard} rests on one pair at equal disk ($-1.6\,\%$); the declared series says $-12.3\,\%$ under a threshold of \SI{26.8}{\percent} (noise \SI{11.9}{\percent}), not real either way.
\item \textbf{The agreement with Volume~I} ($h = 0.591$ against \num{0.590} predicted) may be a coincidence of a scenario fitted on one point.
\item \textbf{C12's text and the harmony conversation} are greedy: they show that the engine produces coherent text, not its quality; the judge is C4.
\end{itemize}
\end{faux}
