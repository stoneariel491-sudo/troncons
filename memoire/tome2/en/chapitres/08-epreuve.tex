\section{The protocol}\label{t2e-sec:protocole}

The architecture wrote the trial's protocol before any construction (\fichier{decisions/ARCHITECTURE.md}, § 8). The judge is an agent who wrote neither the specification nor a line of the judged code; he works from a clean clone, on mains power, with a free card, and reads only the specification, the code and the logs. He follows seven steps: (1) write, \emph{from § 4 of the specification alone}, a decoder of the format and reproduce to the bit chunks drawn at random; (2) replay the breaks and check that every control fails, then holds; (3) redo the numbers in ABBA series; (4) check that the baseline is not badly tuned; (5) check that the memory instrument sees the window; (6) read the text; (7) write his verdict, with his own ``What could be wrong'' section.

\paragraph{The conditions, recorded.} Mains power at every pass; free card at the start (\SI{346}{\Mio}); card lock taken at 02:26 and released at 04:44. Foreign load recorded and left alone: a \texttt{find} from another agent (one core), an \texttt{nvidia-smi} monitor running since the day before, and an orphan \texttt{nvidia-smi} born at 01:38 (it would become finding G2). Build from a clone at commit \texttt{527dff5}, in \SI{21}{\second}, \emph{without} the SOCLE's recipe (which broke: finding m2). The judge opened the format crates only \emph{after} step 1, and his decoder took nothing from them. \SI{128}{\Go} were left free on the disk.

\section{Step 1: is the specification true?}\label{t2e-sec:geste1}

\paragraph{The tool.} \fichier{decodeur\_s4.py} (Python 3.13, numpy): a GGUF v3 reader written from the public specification; the \texttt{.trc} header field by field; the META, RESIDENTS, TABLES and DIRECTORY sections compared with the GGUF; and, per chunk, the part header, the stream table, the rANS decoding of § 4.9.2, the per-lane table of § 4.9.3 and the exact reconstruction of § 4.11. Every rebuilt expert is compared with the original digest of the directory and with the GGUF bytes; two checks the decoder does not require are added (median equal to \texttt{sort[B/2]}, end of every tile equal to the start of the next).

\paragraph{The vectorised decoder.} An rANS stream of the specification is cut into independent tiles of 32 lanes; an expert has 127 of them, and each stream decodes in \num{1024} sequential steps. The judge decodes the 127 tiles at once, lane by lane in parallel: \SI{0.8}{\second} per expert instead of minutes. One must then prove that this vectorised form is indeed the normative decoding, which reads the words ``for $l = 0$ to 31, in order''.

\begin{lemme}[Vectorised rANS is the rANS of § 4.9.2]\label{t2e-lem:vectorise}
Within a tile, let $k$ be the sub-step of macro-step $m$, $p$ the current position in the stream of 16-bit words, and for each lane $l$ the intermediate state $y_l$ after the decoding step. Normative decoding makes lane $l$ renormalise if and only if $y_l < L = 2^{16}$, reading one word. It is equivalent to compute $b_l = [y_l < L]$ for all 32 lanes at once, to make every renormalising lane read the word of index $p + \sum_{l' \le l} b_{l'} - 1$, and then to set $p \leftarrow p + \sum_l b_l$.
\end{lemme}
\begin{proof}
In the normative order, lane $l$ reads, if it renormalises, the word following those read by lanes $l' < l$ in the same sub-step, i.e.\ word $p + \#\{l' < l : y_{l'} < L\} = p + \sum_{l' \le l} b_{l'} - 1$ when $b_l = 1$. It remains to see that no lane renormalises twice in the same sub-step, otherwise the normative order would read two words for one lane. Before the step the state satisfies $x \ge L$; the step gives $y = f_s \,(x \gg 12) + (x \bmod 2^{12}) - c_s$ with $f_s \ge 1$, hence $y \ge x \gg 12 \ge 2^{16} \gg 12 = 16$. If $y < L$, renormalisation gives $(y \ll 16) \mid w \ge 16 \cdot 2^{16} \ge L$: one word always suffices. Each tile has its own start and states, so tiles are independent.
\end{proof}

\section{A third double-precision forward pass}\label{t2e-sec:numpy}

\paragraph{Why a third computation.} C4 judges troncons against a ``truth'': lot R's f64 forward pass. Were that truth wrong, C4 would be worth nothing. The judge wrote another one, in numpy f64, from the model form published by OpenAI (reference implementation under Apache-2.0, \cite{openai2025gptossref}; nothing copied), without reading anything of lot R. It comprises the Q8\_0 embedding, RMSNorm, the projections and their biases, half-split RoPE with the real YaRN ramp and the concentration $0.1 \ln 32 + 1$, attention with 64 query heads and 8 key heads, the $1/8$ scale, the per-head sink in the denominator and a 128-key window on even layers, the top-4 router then the softmax over the four, the SwiGLU clamped at 7, the MXFP4 experts dequantised exactly, and the output head. The ramp bounds it finds, \num{8.09278} and \num{17.39802}, equal lot R's. Duration: \SI{6}{\minute} for the 20b, \SI{17}{\minute} for the 120b, over 511 positions.

\paragraph{The reference against the judge's pass.} Over the 255 scored positions of window 0 of the 20b, and the \num{201088} tokens of each, the maximum gap of log-probabilities above the format's floor is \num{0.000123} nat, i.e.\ \textbf{\num{0.504} quantization step} (median step \num{2.44e-4}); 255 positions out of 255 under one step; \num{100}\,\% identical top choices \mes. This number has a theoretical bound.

\begin{proposition}[The gap due to the format alone]\label{t2e-prop:format}
A \texttt{.kld} file codes, per position, $\log q_i = \text{step} \cdot \text{code}_i + \log q_{\min}$ with an integer code rounded to nearest, and gives code 0 to every logit under the floor. If two computations return the same real $\log q_i$, the values read from their files differ by at most $\text{step}/2$ on every token above the floor, plus the f32 rounding of step and $\log q_{\min}$ (of the order of $10^{-6}$ relative).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Rounding to nearest moves a value by at most half a step; the value read is $\text{step} \cdot \text{code} + \log q_{\min}$, computed from two f32 numbers stored in the file, whose rounding adds its own term.
\end{proof}

Measured: \num{0.504} step, i.e.\ $\text{step}/2$ plus \num{0.004} step. Lot R's reference thus equals the judge's pass \emph{up to the rounding of the format, and nothing else}. llama.cpp's ramp, for its part, departs from it by up to \SI{6.5}{nat} on some tokens.

\paragraph{Why an exact KL is negative.} The tokens under the format's floor (about \num{85000} out of \num{201088}) all receive $\log \tilde q = \log q_{\min}$, larger than their true value: the distribution read, $\tilde q$, is not normalised, $\sum_i \tilde q_i = e^{\delta}$, with $\delta = \num{0.0020}$ on average \mes.

\begin{lemme}[The KL of an exact computation against a file]\label{t2e-lem:kl-negative}
For a computation identical to the truth, $\mathrm{KL}(p\,\|\,\tilde q) = \sum_i p_i (\log p_i - \log \tilde q_i)$ tends to $-\sum_{i \,\text{under the floor}} p_i\,(\log q_{\min} - \log p_i) < 0$.
\end{lemme}
\begin{proof}
Above the floor, $\log \tilde q_i = \log p_i$ to within half a step (\cref{t2e-prop:format}), and these gaps, of mixed signs, cancel on average. Under the floor, $\log \tilde q_i = \log q_{\min} > \log p_i$, and every term $p_i(\log p_i - \log q_{\min})$ is negative.
\end{proof}

Measured: \num{-4.8e-4} for lot R's f64 reference against the judge's truth. This bias, common to all files, changes no ordering; it sets the zero of the instrument, and it is exactly the KL the f32 KV cache would later reach (\cref{t2res-sec:kv}).

\paragraph{C4, at last judged on the 120b.} Against this truth, troncons does \num{0.00308} and \num{95.7}\,\% of top choices on window 0 of the 120b, llama.cpp \num{0.0607} and \num{80.4}\,\%; paired difference $-\num{0.0576} \pm \num{0.0120}$, disjoint Wilson intervals. The detail is in \cref{t2res-tab:c4}. On the same window, the YaRN ramp alone explains \num{55}\,\% of llama.cpp's gap on the 20b: lot O's ``six times closer'' is true, but it bears mostly on a modelling choice (finding m5).

\section{Is the baseline honest?}\label{t2e-sec:base}

The judge ran the best published configuration (\texttt{fit}, 6 threads) and another of his choice, \texttt{fit -t 10}, in the same session; his reason, declared beforehand: under \texttt{mmap}, every thread in a page fault is a read in flight, so more threads make a deeper queue on the disk. Result: \num{0.627} token/s at 6 threads (noise \SI{3.0}{\percent}), \textbf{\num{0.702}} at 10 threads (noise \SI{0.6}{\percent}); a gap of \num{11.9}\,\%, real; same texts. The published baseline underestimated llama.cpp, and lot O's $\times 3.85$ had to be struck out (finding M2). The judge himself wrote what he had not done: neither 12 nor 16 threads, nor \texttt{--n-cpu-moe}. The round did it; the result is in \cref{t2res-sec:base}: at 16 threads, \num{0.884}, and the gap was \num{54}\,\%, not 12.

\section{The round of corrections}\label{t2e-sec:ronde}

The round declared its first threshold on 16 October at 06:12 (commit \texttt{ca5d338}), seven minutes after the last commit of the trial's report; its final state is dated 10:15 (\fichier{moteur/REPRISE-O.md}, commit \texttt{30d8165}). Its rules were the project's, applied to every finding: \emph{reproduce first} the defect with the previous binary (kept, a frozen copy of commit \texttt{7b288a6}), declare the threshold before the pass (\fichier{mesures/moteur/O/corrections/seuils-declares.md}, one block committed per finding), correct, then show that the control fails with the old binary and holds with the new one. The break of the control is the old binary.

\subsection{G1: the hung pass}\label{t2e-sec:g1}

\paragraph{What the judge saw.} The \texttt{evaluer --flux --brut} pass over the ten windows of the 120b, launched at 03:33 while the judge's numpy pass was reading \SI{61}{\Go} and occupying the processor, never finished. After \SI{35}{\minute}: no further read in \SI{10}{\second} (\num{1863} reads, \SI{18.06}{\Go}), card at \SI{0}{\percent}, main thread at \SI{100}{\percent}, and not one line on the error output. The judge sampled the main thread's instruction pointer 300 times (300 out of 300 in \texttt{nvcuda64.dll}) then named its stack through the symbols: \texttt{troncons::principal} $\to$ destruction of the \texttt{Moteur} $\to$ \texttt{Arc<Interne>::drop\_slow} $\to$ destruction of the CUDA events $\to$ \texttt{nvcuda64.dll}. The engine had left its loop, very probably on an error, and its destruction was waiting for work left queued on the card; the error itself was never written. Relaunched alone, the same command finishes in \SI{287}{\second}.

\paragraph{The corrected exit} (\cref{t2e-fig:sortie}). On any error, the engine first writes the error (on the error output and in a \fichier{<journal>.erreur.json}), then ends its own process with \texttt{TerminateProcess} \cite{ms-terminateprocess}, without destroying the engine or the CUDA context: the driver frees everything at the end of the process. A heartbeat per prefill layer dates any future hang.

\paragraph{A probable cause, found by reading the code.} In stream prefill, the reads of a batch took their pinned arrival buffers round robin, $s \bmod (d \cdot k)$ with $d \cdot k = 12$, with at most $\min(d\cdot k, 2 \cdot \text{depth}) = 8$ reads in flight, handed to the system four at a time (\cref{t2e-fig:tampons}).

\begin{figure}[ht]
\centering
\SchemaResTampons
\caption[The round robin of arrival buffers]{The round robin of arrival buffers in stream prefill, and the counterexample of \cref{t2e-lem:tampons}: read $s-12$ stays in flight while $s-11, \dots, s-1$ go through and are reaped; read $s$ would take its buffer.}
\label{t2e-fig:tampons}
\end{figure}

\begin{lemme}[The arrival buffers of stream prefill]\label{t2e-lem:tampons}
(i) With the rule from before the round, a read could write into a buffer that another read was still writing. (ii) With the corrected rule (take the round-robin buffer if it holds no read in flight, otherwise the next that holds none), no read writes into a buffer $i$ while (a) another read is writing it, (b) the copy to the card that was reading it has not finished, (c) a hash of the guard is still reading it.
\end{lemme}
\begin{proof}
(i) Counterexample. Completions arrive out of order. Let read $s-12$ stay in the system while $s-11, \dots, s-1$ go through the three other places and are reaped: the local list of reads in flight then holds only $s-12$, of length $1 < 8$, and read $s$ is submitted into buffer $s \bmod 12 = (s-12) \bmod 12$, which the disk is still writing. The copy launched when $s-12$ is reaped then reads a mixture: wrong lane check (fatal error, followed by G1's destruction) or, if only raw bytes are touched, a wrong expert without an error. It suffices that one read lags the others by about $11 \times \SI{6.6}{\milli\second} / 3 \approx \SI{24}{\milli\second}$ (\SI{12}{\Mo} at \SI{1.8}{\Go\per\second}, three places) \calc; the 90th percentile of a cold read recorded by the judge, \SI{172}{\milli\second}, exceeds it.
(ii) Existence: at the moment of the choice, at most $\min(d\cdot k, 2 \cdot \text{depth}) - 1 < d \cdot k$ reads are in flight, so at least one buffer holds none. (a) follows from the rule. (b): submission waits for the event of the buffer's previous copy, queued when it was reaped. (c): submission first waits for the end of the zone's hashes. The corresponding VRAM buffer is rewritten only after the end of the decode that was reading it.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Replayed under the same load.} Block 4 of the declared thresholds replayed the hung pass under the trial's load (the f64 numpy pass of the 120b, \SI{61}{\Go} read, launched first). The new binary finishes in \SI{361}{\second}, with 360 heartbeats, a \texttt{.kld} equal to lot O's and \textbf{0 arrival conflicts}: the lemma's case did not occur. The old binary, under the same load, \textbf{also finishes} (\SI{338}{\second}, equal \texttt{.kld}). The trial's hang is thus not reproduced: it depends on an order of completions or a machine state that two replays did not recover. What is known: the destruction could wait forever (named stack); the new binary destroys nothing on an error and writes it first; the round robin could overwrite a read in flight (proved). What is not known: the original error.

\subsection{G2: the orphan that holds the pipes}\label{t2e-sec:g2}

The engine's VRAM sampler launched \texttt{nvidia-smi -lms 250} and killed it only in its normal stop; no destruction did. Any error left the child alive. And it inherited the engine's handles, hence the driver's output pipes: the driver, which waits for the outputs to close, waited as long as the orphan lived, that is, forever. In the trial, two passes finished in a few seconds ``gave back control'' only after 518 and \SI{630}{\second}, when the judge killed their orphans; an orphan born at 01:38 from a lot O pass was still running at 04:40.

\paragraph{The correction, in four moves.} (1) A destruction of the sampler that kills and waits for the child; (2) the child placed in a Windows ``kill on close'' job \cite{ms-jobobject-limit}, which kills it even if the parent dies by \texttt{TerminateProcess}, which the destruction does not see; (3) a null standard input; (4) the parent's standard handles made non-inheritable, because a true \texttt{bInheritHandles} gives the child every inheritable handle \cite{ms-createprocess}, the driver's pipes among them.

\paragraph{The control.} \fichier{corrections/temoin\_sortie.py} launches three cases as the drivers do (missing file, corrupted file, killed process), outputs in pipes, and waits for them to close. Declared threshold: return within \SI{30}{\second}, code 1, error written, no \texttt{nvidia-smi} alive \SI{2}{\second} later. The old binary \textbf{fails} on all three (return at the \SI{90}{\second} timeout, one orphan each); the new one \textbf{holds} (\num{1.5}, \num{4.9} and \SI{4.9}{\second}, 0 orphans). The control itself had two defects before it was valid, published: the first pass killed the process before the child was born (it proved nothing); the second did not drain the pipes, and the child blocked on its full error output (two reader threads corrected it).

\subsection{M1: the production guard}\label{t2e-sec:garde}

\paragraph{The hole.} In production, the only check on a chunk was the return of the rANS lanes to $L$. It guards only the \emph{coded} streams: the raw streams of a part (MEDIANS, BIASES: about \SI{43}{\kilo\octet} out of \SI{12}{\Mo}, \SI{0.36}{\percent}) and, in mode A0, the embedding row read at every token were guarded by nothing. On the copy with a wrong median, the text produced was \emph{identical} to that of the sound file: a corruption invisible down to the output.

\paragraph{The guard} (\cref{t2e-fig:garde}). At every completed read, the useful bytes of the chunk are hashed by a worker thread and compared with the digest the directory already holds (no seam touched); hashing runs at the same time as the copy and the decode, which only read the buffer; the step waits for all its hashes before returning its token. Embedding rows are compared with a table of digests built at opening by reading \texttt{token\_embd} once, its whole digest checked on the way.

\begin{figure}[ht]
\centering
\SchemaResGarde
\caption[The production guard]{The production guard: hashing runs in parallel with the copy and the decode, on four threads; a barrier joins it before the step returns its token.}
\label{t2e-fig:garde}
\end{figure}

\begin{proposition}[No output depends on an unchecked byte]\label{t2e-prop:garde}
With the guard active, the token and logits returned by a step, or a prefill, depend only on bytes read whose 64-bit digest equals the directory's (chunks: useful bytes of level 0; rows: table built at opening), up to a collision of the 64-bit prefix of SHA-256 \cite{fips180-4}.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
(1) The decoder and the copy read only the useful bytes of a part; padding is read by no kernel. (2) Every completed read is handed to the guard before any other operation on its buffer. (3) No read writes that buffer before the hash ends (\cref{t2e-lem:tampons}, (c)): the digest covers the bytes the copy read. (4) The step and the prefill wait for all hashes before reading the token or the logits; a different digest is a named fatal error. (5) A warm entry served without a read was hashed when it was read; a later corruption of RAM is not covered. (6) Every row read is compared with its slot before it is copied to the card. Wrong content passes with probability $2^{-64}$ per check, if SHA-256 behaves as a random function.
\end{proof}

\begin{faux}\label{t2e-sec:faux}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The judge's numpy pass and lot R's reference read the same GGUF.} A dequantisation error common to both (nibble order, E8M0 scale) would pass. Safeguards: the judge's MXFP4 decoder follows ggml's convention (low nibbles for elements 0 to 15), and C3 like the texts tie everything else together to the bit; the convention of the format is judged only by agreement with llama.cpp, not by OpenAI.
\item \textbf{They follow the same reading of the model} (real ramp, sinks, 128-wide window). Had OpenAI trained with another convention, both would be wrong together; OpenAI's \texttt{config.json} and reference implementation are the only sources of this reading.
\item \textbf{C4 on the 120b rests on one window}; the intervals are disjoint, but one window is not ten.
\item \textbf{The machine's memory loss is global}: other processes move it. It is clean in session \texttt{s1}; it was soiled (\SI{10.5}{\Go}, the numpy pass at the same time) in another pass the judge did not use.
\item \textbf{The judge's instrument had two defects}, found and corrected before any verdict: a process handle passed as a 32-bit integer (a counter control that returned 0), and a first C8 calculation that counted the loading of the residents twice (``the engine counts \SI{1.4}{\Go} too many''), redone correctly and never published.
\item \textbf{The cause of G1 is not established}: the buffer defect is proved possible, not observed (0 conflicts in two replays).
\item \textbf{The guard assumes SHA-256 without a useful collision} on 64 bits; it covers neither warm RAM after its read, nor VRAM.
\item \textbf{The time of one threshold was wrong} in the judge's file: \barre{02:40} 02:26, the commit's time, before the driver's trial run (02:26:35) and the first pass (02:27:40). The thresholds were indeed declared before any pass; only the written time was wrong.
\end{itemize}
\end{faux}
