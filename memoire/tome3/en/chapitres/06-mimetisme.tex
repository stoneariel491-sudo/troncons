\begin{enbref}
Mimicry asks other disciplines how they solved a problem that resembles ours: keeping in memory what will serve again, letting the rest go, deciding fast under uncertainty. The image is that of a borrowing: a climatologist knows that a slow reservoir integrates the forcing of a fast reservoir; a physicist knows that a spin glass has valleys where a greedy descent stops; a statistician knows how to guarantee a precision without knowing the law of the data. None of these resemblances is a proof: each proposes a measurement, and it is the measurement that decides. Out of six leads, none clearly ``holds'' under its judge; four remain open for a precise reason, put in numbers (traces too short or degenerate, an unmeasured overlap, an unvalidated simulator, synthetic weights), and two say something clear: an inclusive RAM stage smaller than the VRAM is of no use, and a router that favours the present experts removes 10\,\% of the misses for a KL of the order of 0.03, at the edge of what is admitted.
\end{enbref}

\section{The sorting: forty-five candidates, six leads}\label{t3mim:sec:tri}

\paragraph{Where the candidates come from.} Each scout read its domain and proposed mechanisms transposable to the path of the weights, with, when it was possible without the card, a first measurement on the traces of the engine. The sorting (\fichier{genome/mimetisme/tri.md}, 2026-10-27) removed their duplicates, ranked them, and wrote for each of the six leads retained a complete protocol: data, quantity, threshold, controls, second path, cost. The thresholds of the sorting are committed before the document (\fichier{mesures/mimetisme/tri/00-seuils-declares.md}, commit \texttt{ec124f5}); a sorting measures nothing. The strongest signal is the long-memory LFU: it comes out of four scouts positively (life, Earth and cosmos, human, engineering) and out of two negatively (physics: the shape of the kernel does not matter; mathematics: a learnt predictor adds only $+0.012$). But all its measurements had been made on \emph{reading} traces: the decaying LFU had never been measured on a generation, which is the real load. And the ``LFU $-0.075$ in generation'' of engineering was not the counter-example it seemed: its ``lfu'' has no decay (the counter rises, only the ``adm'' policy halves it, read in \fichier{outils/mimetisme/ingenierie/cache2.py}). The question remained open, hence P1.

\paragraph{Two families.} Three of the six leads touch the bottleneck without touching quality (P1, P2, P4: fewer bytes read for the same output to the bit); three trade quality for bytes (P3, P5, P6) and are judged by the KL. The latter share a bar: the slope of the fast mode, 0.13 of KL for $-34\,\%$ of bytes, linearised as 0.038 for 10 points; P6 was to provide the curve KL$(f)$ that this slope lacked.

\paragraph{What is ruled out, and why.} The sorting rules out some thirty candidates, each by a number or a written reason (\fichier{tri.md} §\,4). The most telling: the physical memory kernels (Omori--Utsu, Kohlrausch), closed by measurement E1 ($+0.0051$, $+0.0028$, $+0.0044$ against a threshold of $+0.015$: the shape does not matter, the half-life does, and it goes into P1); the Fermi cut-off (removing the expert of small weight), closed by E2 (0.09\,\% of the misses below 0.05; the gateway to the fast mode becomes P6); batched verification, killed by its ceiling ($G(4) = 1.44$, $G(8) = 1.84$ for a free draft); the learnt eviction predictor ($+0.0124$ against the best tuned LFU, below $+0.05$); segments with ghost lists (the SLRU with a 20\,\% probation collapses, 0.257 against 0.738); the cache in coded form, dominated by P2 ($-8\,\%$ of bytes for 120\,ms of card per token); and, narrowly, reverse water-filling by usage (0.094 bit achieved against a threshold of 0.126), the best compression lead not retained.

\paragraph{The shared instruments.} Three rules hold for all the leads. (i) The \emph{group sequential stopping} of O'Brien and Fleming \cite{obrien1979}, calibrated by Monte Carlo by the human scout (constant $c = 2.840$): 6.8 windows out of 10 on average over five decisive series, with a floor of 6 windows, because one series crossed the boundary at 4 windows ($t = -5.1$) and was no longer decisive at 10 ($t = -2.1$). (ii) The \emph{pairing rule} of lot R: a mode is better if $\bar d + 2\sigma(\bar d) < 0$, $\sigma$ by blocks of windows. (iii) \emph{Separate prefill and generation in any cache judgement}: admission policies win at reading ($+0.081$) and lose at generation ($-0.094$) (engineering, H3); it is the common trap, and P1 and P4 fell into it otherwise than expected (\cref{t3mim:sec:p1-traces}).

\paragraph{The common form of the six protocols.} Each lead committed its threshold before any reading of data, and each judge checked the order of the commits by \texttt{git log}; each lead has a control whose answer is known in advance and a break that must make it fail; each main number has a second path; and each lead was redone by an independent judge, written without the code of the lead. The verdict of this chapter is always that of the judge.

\section{P1: the two-scale cache memory}\label{t3mim:sec:p1}

\paragraph{The idea.} A cache of experts decides whom to keep. The LRU keeps the most recent; the LFU, the most frequent. Human memory forgets as a power law \cite{anderson1991reflections}, and the climate has two reservoirs, a fast one that follows the forcing and a slow one that integrates it \cite{hasselmann1976stochastic}; the ``cache'' version of this idea is the LRFU \cite{lee2001lrfu}, a score that decays with a half-life. If such a score wins in generation, it is the cheapest change of the project: one counter per expert, 4\,608 floats for the 120b. The four records are reread through Crossref on 2026-10-29; the papers are not, and nothing in the verdict depends on them.

\subsection{The exponentially decaying score, a rate estimator}\label{t3mim:sec:p1-score}

\begin{definition}[Half-life score]\label{t3mim:def:score}
Let an expert be accessed at tokens $s_1 < s_2 < \dots$ For a half-life $h$ (in tokens), its score at token $t$ is
\begin{equation}\label{t3mim:eq:score}
c_h(t) = \sum_{s_i \le t} 2^{-(t - s_i)/h}, \qquad \text{updated by } c \leftarrow c \cdot 2^{-\Delta t / h} + 1 .
\end{equation}
The cache evicts the resident with the smallest score. We set $\tau = h / \ln 2$, the time constant.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemme}[Expectation and variance]\label{t3mim:lem:esperance}
If the accesses form a Poisson process of rate $\lambda$ per token, $\E[c_h] = \lambda h / \ln 2$ and $\operatorname{Var}[c_h] = \lambda h / (2\ln 2)$. So $\hat r_h = c_h \ln 2 / h$ is an unbiased estimator of $\lambda$, with variance $\lambda \ln 2 / (2h) = \lambda/(2\tau)$.
\end{lemme}
\begin{proof}
The score is a Poisson process filtered by the kernel $k(u) = 2^{-u/h} = e^{-u/\tau}$. By Campbell's formula, $\E[c_h] = \lambda \int_0^\infty e^{-u/\tau}\,\dd u = \lambda\tau$ and $\operatorname{Var}[c_h] = \lambda \int_0^\infty e^{-2u/\tau}\,\dd u = \lambda\tau/2$. Multiplying by $\ln 2/h = 1/\tau$: $\E[\hat r_h] = \lambda$ and $\operatorname{Var}[\hat r_h] = \lambda/(2\tau)$.
\end{proof}
The relative deviation, $\sqrt{\ln 2 / (2h\lambda)}$, is the price of a short memory: at $\lambda = 0.1$ access per token and $h = 8$, it is $\sqrt{0.6931/1.6} = 0.66$ \calc.

\begin{lemme}[Lag bias]\label{t3mim:lem:retard}
If the rate drifts linearly, $\lambda(t) = \lambda_0 + \lambda' t$, then $\E[\hat r_h(t)] = \lambda(t) - \lambda'\tau$, and the mean squared error is $\mathrm{MSE}(\tau) = \lambda/(2\tau) + \lambda'^2\tau^2$.
\end{lemme}
\begin{proof}
$\E[\hat r_h(t)] = \int_0^\infty \lambda(t-u)\,e^{-u/\tau}\,\dd u/\tau = \lambda(t) - \lambda' \int_0^\infty u\,e^{-u/\tau}\,\dd u/\tau = \lambda(t) - \lambda'\tau$. The MSE is the variance of \cref{t3mim:lem:esperance} plus the square of the bias.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Optimal horizon]\label{t3mim:prop:horizon}
For $0 < \lambda' < \infty$, the MSE is minimal at $\tau^\ast = \bigl(\lambda/(4\lambda'^2)\bigr)^{1/3}$, which is neither $0$ (the LRU) nor $\infty$ (the LFU).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$\dd\,\mathrm{MSE}/\dd\tau = -\lambda/(2\tau^2) + 2\lambda'^2\tau$ vanishes at $\tau^3 = \lambda/(4\lambda'^2)$; the second derivative $\lambda/\tau^3 + 2\lambda'^2$ is positive.
\end{proof}
The horizon grows when the drift weakens and when the rate is low. The measurement places the best $h$, chosen on the readings of the other genres, at $h^\ast = 32$ ($\tau = 46$ tokens) at 326 slots, and at 32 or 64 at 922 \mes~(\fichier{mesures/mimetisme/P1/juge-120b-*.json}, field \texttt{cv}).

\begin{proposition}[The LRU--LFU continuum]\label{t3mim:prop:continuum}
As $h \to 0$, the order of the scores tends to the order of the last accesses (LRU); as $h \to \infty$, to the order of the counts (LFU).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$c_h(t) = 2^{-(t - s_{\text{last}})/h}\bigl(1 + \sum_{i \ne \text{last}} 2^{-(s_{\text{last}} - s_i)/h}\bigr)$. As $h \to 0$, the second factor tends to 1 and the first orders by $s_{\text{last}}$; as $h \to \infty$, each term of the sum \eqref{t3mim:eq:score} tends to 1 and the score to the count.
\end{proof}
This is the LRFU family \cite{lee2001lrfu}. The controls check it to the bit: at $h = 0.001$ the hit rate equals that of the LRU to within 0 on eight traces; at $h = \infty$ it equals the ``lfu'' of \texttt{cache2} to within 0 \mes~(\fichier{mesures/mimetisme/P1/temoins.json}).

\paragraph{Two reservoirs.} The mixture $M(w) = (1-w)\,\hat r_8 + w\,\hat r_{128}$ is the output of a linear filter with impulse response $k(u) = (1-w)\tfrac{\ln 2}{8}2^{-u/8} + w\tfrac{\ln 2}{128}2^{-u/128}$, a sum of two relaxations, with transfer function
\begin{equation}\label{t3mim:eq:transfert}
K(s) = \frac{1-w}{1 + 8s/\ln 2} + \frac{w}{1 + 128 s/\ln 2},
\end{equation}
two real poles: it is Hasselmann's two-reservoir structure, stated here as an analogy of form. $M(0) = \mathrm{LFU}(8)$ and $M(1) = \mathrm{LFU}(128)$ to within 0 (control W3). The mixture is not better than the LFU(32) alone: $-0.0031$, $+0.1042$, $-0.0657$, $+0.0892$ against $-0.0176$, $+0.1136$, $-0.0449$, $+0.0981$ on the four cases of the verdict \mes.

\paragraph{From hit rate to speed.} If the disk bounds everything, the time of a token is proportional to the misses, $v \propto 1/(1-s)$, and a cache of hit rate $s_1$ against $s_0$ speeds up by a factor
\begin{equation}\label{t3mim:eq:vitesse}
\rho = \frac{1 - s_0}{1 - s_1}.
\end{equation}
The declared threshold ($+0.02$ of hit rate on 0.2621 of misses) is worth $\rho = 0.2621/0.2421 = 1.083$; the wiki generation ($0.3944 \to 0.5080$) would be worth $0.6056/0.4920 = 1.23$, but on a loop; a cold window of the 120b (LRU 0.45, $\bar d = +0.094$) would be worth $0.55/0.456 = 1.21$ \calc. (The note of the lead writes $\rho \approx 1.19$ for this last case; the computation set out gives 1.21.)

\section{P2: the exclusive cache from VRAM to RAM}\label{t3mim:sec:p2}

\paragraph{The idea.} In a storage hierarchy, when the upper level evicts a block, it can \emph{demote} it into the lower level instead of duplicating it: this is the exclusive hierarchy of Wong and Wilkes \cite{wong2002exclusive}. The cache of the engine does the opposite: in \texttt{Cache::demander}, the RAM is filled at each disk miss and refreshed at each VRAM hit (read by the sorting and by the judge in \fichier{moteur/crates/trc-cache/src/lib.rs}, lines 153 to 241). It therefore contains the most recent experts, already in VRAM. The simulation of the scout said so without appeal: the inclusive cache with $R \le V$ adds 0.0000 of hit rate.

\subsection{The LRU stack and the theorem}\label{t3mim:sec:p2-theoreme}

\begin{definition}[Stack distance]\label{t3mim:def:pile}
The LRU stack is the list of the experts already seen, from the most recent (rank 1) to the oldest. The stack distance of an access to $x$ is the rank of $x$ in the stack just before the access (infinite at the first access). We write $F(C)$ for the share of accesses with stack distance at most $C$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemme}[Inclusion, Mattson et al.]\label{t3mim:lem:mattson}
An LRU cache of capacity $C$ contains, at every instant, the first $C$ elements of the LRU stack. An access is a hit in it if and only if its stack distance is at most $C$; the hit rate of an LRU of capacity $C$ is therefore $F(C)$.
\end{lemme}
\begin{proof}
By induction on the accesses. An access of distance $d \le C$ brings $x$ to the top; ranks $1, \dots, d-1$ slide down one place, the set of the first $C$ ranks is unchanged: a hit. An access of distance $d > C$ brings $x$ to the top and pushes rank $C$ to rank $C+1$, which is exactly the least recent of the cache, the one the LRU evicts: a miss.
\end{proof}
This is the property of the stack algorithms of Mattson, Gecsei, Slutz and Traiger \cite{mattson1970} (record reread through Crossref by lot O on 2026-10-15; the paper is not reread here, and the proof above does not rely on it).

\begin{theoreme}[The two-stage exclusive cache is worth an LRU of capacity $V + R$]\label{t3mim:thm:exclusif}
Let a VRAM stage of capacity $V$ and a RAM stage of capacity $R$ be given, both LRU. On a VRAM miss, the VRAM victim is demoted to the top of the RAM; if the requested expert is in RAM, it is promoted and leaves the RAM; if the RAM overflows, its oldest disappears. Then, at every instant, the VRAM contains ranks $1, \dots, V$ of the stack, the RAM ranks $V+1, \dots, V+R$, and the total hit rate is $F(V+R)$.
\end{theoreme}
\begin{proof}
By induction on the accesses; true for the empty stack. Consider an access to $x$ of rank $d$.
\emph{If $d \le V$}, $x$ goes to the top, ranks $1, \dots, d-1$ slide down one place; the set of ranks $1, \dots, V$ is unchanged, and so is the RAM: a VRAM hit.
\emph{If $V < d \le V + R$}, $x$ is in RAM; it is promoted and leaves it. The old rank $V$ becomes rank $V+1$: it is the VRAM victim (its oldest), which enters at the top of the RAM, in its place of rank $V+1$. The old ranks $V+1, \dots, d-1$ slide to ranks $V+2, \dots, d$, the ranks beyond $d$ do not move: the RAM is exactly ranks $V+1, \dots, V+R$ of the new stack. A RAM hit.
\emph{If $d > V + R$} (or at the first access), $x$ goes to the top, the VRAM victim (rank $V$) becomes rank $V+1$ and enters at the top of the RAM; the RAM overflows by one element, its oldest, which is rank $V+R+1$ of the new stack, and it leaves. A disk miss.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollaire}[The stack band]\label{t3mim:cor:bande}
The share served by the RAM is $F(V+R) - F(V)$, the disk share $1 - F(V+R)$. The gain of the exclusive cache is the mass of the stack distances lying in the band $(V, V+R]$.
\end{corollaire}

With the pinning of the groups, the victim is the oldest \emph{outside the group}, and the equality is no longer exact. Measured (control T2, 7 traces, $R = 80$ and 336): the gap to the LRU of capacity $V+R$ does not exceed $6.6 \times 10^{-4}$; on the 20b without pinning, it is exactly 0 \mes~(\fichier{mesures/mimetisme/P2/holdout20b-t2-sans-epinglage.json}). \Cref{fig:t3-mim-p2-pile} shows the theorem on the two generations: the gain is the area under $F$ between $V$ and $V+R$, 0.08 on prose-en where the curve is almost flat after $V$, 0.32 on wiki whose working set straddles the boundary.

\paragraph{The inclusive cache, seen through the same stack.} The judge measured that an inclusive cache whose RAM has $R' = V + R = 662$ entries has \emph{exactly} the disk share of the exclusive cache at $R = 336$ (0.184489 and 0.288493 on the two generations, pinning included), without any demotion \mes~(\fichier{mesures/mimetisme/P2/juge/budget-egal.json}). The same stack argument explains it; the following proposition is set out by the writing, under a hypothesis on the rule of the engine that the judge read in the code but that is not measured in itself.

\begin{proposition}[The inclusive cache with RAM $R' \ge V$]\label{t3mim:prop:inclusif}
Suppose, without pinning, that the inclusive RAM receives each expert read from the disk, that the date of each of its entries is refreshed at each access (served by the VRAM or by it), and that it evicts its oldest entry. If $R' \ge V$, the RAM contains at every instant ranks $1, \dots, R'$ of the stack, and the total hit rate is $F(R')$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By induction; true at the start, as long as the RAM is not full (each expert seen entered it at its first access). Suppose the RAM equal to the first $R'$ ranks; since $V \le R'$, the VRAM (the first $V$ ranks, \cref{t3mim:lem:mattson}) is included in it. An access of rank $d \le R'$ is served (by the VRAM if $d \le V$, by the RAM otherwise) and refreshes the entry of $x$ in the RAM: the RAM remains the first $R'$ ranks of the new stack. An access of rank $d > R'$ is a disk miss; $x$ enters the RAM and its oldest entry, the old rank $R'$, now rank $R'+1$, leaves.
\end{proof}
Hence, at equal capacities in entries, $1 - F(662)$ for the inclusive cache at 662 as for the exclusive $(326, 336)$. For $R < V$ the argument does not go through: an entry of the RAM can age while the VRAM serves experts absent from the RAM. The lead gives only a sketch, and the measurement stands in for it: 0 RAM hits at $R = 80$ and 160 on the 7 traces (\fichier{mesures/mimetisme/P2/grille.csv}, inclusive mode), at most $7.65 \times 10^{-4}$ at $R = V = 326$ \mes~(\fichier{mesures/mimetisme/P2/t1bord.csv}).

\subsection{The cost of demotion}\label{t3mim:sec:p2-cout}

A disk miss costs $a = 4.1$\,ms (11.6\,MB at 2.82\,GB/s); a miss served by the decoded RAM $b = 1.09$\,ms (13.25\,MB at 12.2\,GB/s); a demotion from VRAM to RAM costs $b$ too \calc. Let $\varphi$ be the number of VRAM misses per token and $\rho$ the share of them served by the RAM; each VRAM miss causes a demotion. Per token:
\begin{align}
T_{\text{LRU}} &= \varphi\,a, \nonumber\\
T_{\text{serial}} &= \varphi\,\bigl[(1-\rho)\,a + \rho\,b + b\bigr], \nonumber\\
T_{\text{overlapped}} &= \varphi\,\bigl[(1-\rho)\,a + \rho\,b\bigr]. \label{t3mim:eq:p2-cout}
\end{align}
The serial gain is $\varphi\,[\rho(a-b) - b]$, positive if and only if
\begin{equation}\label{t3mim:eq:p2-seuil}
\rho > \frac{b}{a-b} = \frac{1.09}{3.01} = 0.362 .
\end{equation}
With a fraction $\omega$ of the demotion overlapped by the computation, profitability requires $\rho(a-b) \ge (1-\omega)\,b$, that is $\omega \ge 1 - \rho(a-b)/b$. The measured times say it directly: on prose-en, the LRU alone costs 154.7\,ms per token, the overlapped exclusive 121.1\,ms and the demotion 39.75\,ms, so one needs $\omega \ge 1 - 33.6/39.75 = 0.154$ \calc; $\rho \approx 0.30$ is below threshold \eqref{t3mim:eq:p2-seuil} there, and $\rho \approx 0.52$ on wiki above it.

\section{P3: cache-conditioned routing, bounded by the margin}\label{t3mim:sec:p3}

\paragraph{The idea.} Skliar et al. add a bias to the logits of the experts already in cache before the top-$k$ of the router \cite{skliar2025cacheconditional}. Three scouts reached it by other paths: the seismic fault that sticks (rate-and-state friction), the substitution of an out-of-stock item, the power of two choices of queues; these are analogies, and the measurement alone decides. P3 changes the \emph{stream of requests}, not the policy: Belady is optimal for a fixed stream, and the policy no longer moves (the best predictor exceeds the tuned LFU by $+0.011$ to $+0.013$, mathematics M1). It is also the riskiest of the six: a wanted flip costs like a suffered flip, and the flips carry 96.5\,\% of the KL on the positions that flip (\fichier{genome/05-synthese.md} §\,2.2).

\paragraph{This chapter cites only what is judged.} Stage A (open loop), stage B at $N = 146$, stage B at $N = 238$ and the online biased router in the engine each have their committed judgement. A second draw is in progress in another session (\fichier{genome/mimetisme/P3/REPRISE-second-tirage.md}): none of its numbers is cited. A control at $\beta = 0.3$ outside the engine (\fichier{resultat-temoin-b0.3.md}) has no judgement of its own: it is not cited either.

\subsection{The geometry: a shifted wall, a band}\label{t3mim:sec:p3-geometrie}

A router of gpt-oss-20b returns, per cell (position $t$, layer $\ell$), 32 logits $z \in \mathbb{R}^{32}$; the chosen set $S(z)$ is that of the four largest. In $\mathbb{R}^{32}$, the regions $\{z : S(z) = S_0\}$ are polyhedral cones; their walls are hyperplanes $\{z_i = z_j\}$ of the braid arrangement, and the wall that counts for $S$ is the tropical locus $\{z : z_{(4)} = z_{(5)}\}$, where the \emph{margin} $m(z) = z_{(4)} - z_{(5)}$ vanishes. The margin is, up to $\sqrt 2$, the distance from $z$ to the nearest wall that changes $S$ (\cref{t3tro:ch} makes it its subject).

The bias of Skliar et al. is a \emph{translation} $z' = z + \beta\,\mathbf{1}_R$, where $\mathbf{1}_R$ indicates the resident experts. It does not change the geometry of the regions; it moves the point. For a pair ($a$ absent, $b$ resident), this amounts to shifting the wall $z_a = z_b$ to $z_a = z_b + \beta$ (\cref{fig:t3-mim-p3-paroi}). Every cell whose point falls in the band between the two walls changes its choice: $a$ was taken, $b$ now is. \emph{The share of cells changed is the probability mass of the band.}

\subsection{The decision criterion in one quantity}\label{t3mim:sec:p3-critere}

\begin{proposition}[The threshold on the slope $\kappa$]\label{t3mim:prop:kappa}
Let $f^\ast(N)$ be the share of cells to change to remove 10\,\% of the misses at budget $N$, and suppose the excess KL linear in the share changed, $\mathrm{KL} = \kappa f$ (small-$\beta$ regime). The success threshold (KL $\le \varepsilon = 0.038$ at $-10\,\%$) is equivalent to
\begin{equation}\label{t3mim:eq:kappa}
\kappa \le \kappa_{\max}(N) = \frac{\varepsilon}{f^\ast(N)},
\end{equation}
and the death threshold (KL $> 0.07$) to $\kappa > 0.07/f^\ast(N)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
At the point $f = f^\ast$, $\mathrm{KL} = \kappa f^\ast \le \varepsilon \iff \kappa \le \varepsilon/f^\ast$; likewise for 0.07.
\end{proof}
With $f^\ast(146) = 11.98\,\%$ and $f^\ast(238) = 7.31\,\%$ (interpolated crossings): $\kappa_{\max} = 0.038/0.1198 = 0.317$ and $0.038/0.0731 = 0.520$; death is at $\kappa = 0.584$ and 0.958 \calc. The only $\kappa$ known before stage B was that of the spontaneous flips between two almost exact engines:
\begin{equation}\label{t3mim:eq:kappa0}
\kappa_0 = \frac{0.0145 - 0.00168}{5\,087/122\,640} = \frac{0.01282}{0.04148} = 0.309 ,
\end{equation}

\section{P4: prefetching with calibrated confidence}\label{t3mim:sec:p4}

\paragraph{The idea.} The prefetching of the project died of its \emph{precision}: $p = 0.692$, $+34\,\%$ of bytes, $-11\,\%$ of speed (lot O, §\,9.2), and lot O wrote that it would come back only with $p > 0.9$. Two borrowings: conformal prediction, which guarantees the precision of a subset of predictions without any distributional assumption \cite{angelopoulos2021gentle,angelopoulos2021ltt}; and background traffic that occupies only the free resource and backs off on demand \cite{shalunov2012ledbat}. The predictor is case-based reasoning, like case law: the 20 earlier tokens of the same text whose path of experts most resembles vote for the experts of layer $\ell_0 + d$.

\subsection{The guarantee, set out}\label{t3mim:sec:p4-garantie}

A token $t$ crosses $L$ layers; at layer $\ell$ it asks for $k$ experts. The cache (global LRU) leaves a set of misses. At the end of the routing of $\ell_0$, the predictor proposes for $\ell = \ell_0 + d$ candidates $e$ of confidence $s(e) \in [0, 1]$ (weighted share of the 20 neighbours that carry $e$). A \emph{load} is a candidate absent from the cache with $s \ge \theta$; its precision is $p(\theta) = P(e \in S \mid e \text{ loaded}, s \ge \theta)$, its coverage $u(\theta)$ the share of the misses preceded by a correct load.

\begin{proposition}[Split conformal calibration, fixed sequence]\label{t3mim:prop:conforme}
On the calibration half, let $n(\theta)$ be the loads of confidence $\ge \theta$, of which $x(\theta)$ are correct, and $\mathrm{LB}(\theta) = B^{-1}(\delta;\, x, n - x + 1)$ the one-sided Clopper--Pearson bound \cite{clopper1934} of level $1 - \delta$. One tests $H_\theta : p(\theta) < 0.9$ going down the grid from the highest threshold that retains at least 30 loads, and stops at the first failure; $\theta^\ast$ is the last threshold rejected. If the labels are exchangeable, $P\bigl(p(\theta^\ast) < 0.9\bigr) \le \delta$, without multiplicity correction.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Sketch]
For each fixed $\theta$, $P(\mathrm{LB}(\theta) \ge 0.9 \mid p(\theta) < 0.9) \le \delta$ by construction of the Clopper--Pearson bound. In a fixed sequence stopped at the first failure, a family-wise error requires that the \emph{first} true hypothesis met be wrongly rejected, which happens with probability at most $\delta$; this is the principle of ``Learn then Test'' \cite{angelopoulos2021ltt}.
\end{proof}
Declared setting: $\delta = 0.1$ (one-sided bound at 90\,\%), grid with a step of 0.02 (\fichier{mesures/mimetisme/P4/00-seuils-declares.md}). The exchangeability hypothesis is false for ordered tokens (the history of neighbours grows); the gap goes in the right direction in the four sessions (test precision 0.919, 0.905, 0.947, 0.922 against 0.911, 0.904, 0.918, 0.914 in calibration), without proving it.

\subsection{The simulator and the gain}\label{t3mim:sec:p4-gain}

The time of a layer without prediction is $t_\ell = a + n_d\tau + \mathbb{1}[n_d > 0]\,q + c + o$, with $n_d$ misses, $\tau = 4.1$\,ms per read, $q = 1.0 + 0.85$\,ms (copy, decoding), $a + c = 2.22$\,ms of computation and $o$ a calibration offset (the base takes 460\,ms per token). A background read starts only in a computation window but finishes its course: it delays the next demand by $\delta_r = \tau - (a + c) = 1.9$\,ms. A correct read saves $\tau$; a wrong one costs $\delta_r$. With $U$ correct loads and $C$ loads on the test half:
\begin{equation}\label{t3mim:eq:p4-gain}
\text{saving} = U\tau - C\,\delta_r, \qquad \text{gain} = \frac{\text{saving}}{T_{\text{base}} - \text{saving}} .
\end{equation}
For code: $U = 12\,770$, $C = 13\,892$, $T_{\text{base}} = 516.7\,\mathrm{ms} \times 1\,271$ tokens; saving $= 52\,357 - 26\,395 = 25\,962$\,ms, gain $= 25\,962 / (656\,726 - 25\,962) = 4.12\,\%$ \calc, against 3.58\,\% simulated. When $\delta_r \to 0$ one recovers the formula of the sorting, which overestimates by 2 to 2.4 times. The formula of lot O, $f - (1-p)/p \times \text{disk share}$ with $p = 0.918$, $f = 80/460 = 0.174$ and a disk share of 0.69, gives $+11\,\%$: it assumes that every correct read is hidden in full, which is false when the computation window (2.2\,ms) is shorter than the read (4.1\,ms). Ceiling (perfect predictor): $\times 1.21$ by the computation of lot O.

\section{P5: the choice of the synonym symbols as a spin glass}\label{t3mim:sec:p5}

\paragraph{The idea.} The synonymous mutation of lot B (\cref{t3sno:ch}) puts each expert weight into one of eight levels by minimising a weighted error in a single greedy pass; it is the only lossy gain that has passed the KL ($-0.0098 \pm 0.0033$). The physicist recognises in it the energy of a spin glass, and knows that the greedy pass stops there in a valley; simulated annealing \cite{kirkpatrick1983} and replica exchange \cite{hukushima1996} cross the barriers. It is the domain furthest from computation of all the candidates retained, and the scout found no annealing nor replica exchange applied to the quantisation of a large language model.

\subsection{The problem set out}\label{t3mim:sec:p5-probleme}

A row of an expert matrix has $n = 2\,880$ weights $w_j$, in blocks of 32 sharing a scale $s_b = 2^{e_b - 128}$ (MXFP4). The synonymous mutation puts each weight into one of $k = 8$ levels $\pm c_m s_j$ ($m = 0, \dots, 3$, $c = (0.4743,\ 2.8486,\ 6.8372,\ 12)$ in doubled magnitudes). Let $q$ be the vector of the chosen levels and $r = w - q$ the residual. The output error, averaged over the inputs $x_i$ of the expert weighted by the square $g_i^2$ of the router weight, and the code cost are
\begin{equation}\label{t3mim:eq:p5-energie}
E(q) = r^\top H r + \lambda' \sum_j \ell(q_j), \qquad H = \frac{\sum_i g_i^2\,x_i x_i^\top}{\sum_i g_i^2} + \delta\,\bar m\,I,
\end{equation}
with $\ell(m) = -\log_2 p_m$ the code length of level $m$, $\bar m$ the mean of the diagonal of $H$ without damping and $\delta$ the damping of GPTQ (deployed value $\delta = 1$).

\begin{lemme}[Change of a single weight]\label{t3mim:lem:variation}
If the level of coordinate $j$ is replaced, the residual changes by $\delta r = q_j - q_j'$ at position $j$ only, and
\[
E(q') - E(q) = 2\,\delta r\,(Hr)_j + \delta r^2 H_{jj} + \lambda'\bigl(\ell(q_j') - \ell(q_j)\bigr).
\]
\end{lemme}
\begin{proof}
$r' = r + \delta r\,e_j$, so $r'^\top H r' - r^\top H r = 2\,\delta r\,e_j^\top H r + \delta r^2\,e_j^\top H e_j$.
\end{proof}
Keeping the vector $Hr$ up to date costs $n$ operations per accepted change ($Hr \mathrel{+}= \delta r\,H_{:,j}$); evaluating the $k - 1$ candidates of a coordinate costs $O(k)$. That is what makes the sweeps practicable; the energy kept incrementally equals the energy recomputed from scratch to within $1.3 \times 10^{-15}$ relative \mes.

\paragraph{Three readings of the same object.} (i) \emph{The closest vector.} With $H = VV^\top$, $V$ upper triangular, $r^\top H r = \sum_i \bigl(\sum_{j \le i} V_{ji} r_j\bigr)^2$: term $i$ depends only on $r_1, \dots, r_i$. Choosing $q_1$ that minimises the first term, then $q_2$ given $q_1$, etc., is Babai's nearest-plane algorithm \cite{babai1986}, and GPTQ run from back to front is identical to it \cite{chen2025babai,frantar2022gptq}. Each term is minimised exactly given the past, never looking at the future. (ii) \emph{A spin glass.} Writing the level of $j$ by a Potts state $\sigma_{jm} \in \{0,1\}$, $\sum_m \sigma_{jm} = 1$, $r_j = w_j - s_j \sum_m c_m \sigma_{jm}$,
\begin{equation}\label{t3mim:eq:potts}
E_{\text{err}} = \text{const} - 2\sum_{j,m} h_{jm}\,\sigma_{jm} + \sum_{j,j',m,m'} J^{mm'}_{jj'}\,\sigma_{jm}\sigma_{j'm'}, \quad h_{jm} = s_j c_m (Hw)_j,\ J^{mm'}_{jj'} = H_{jj'} s_j s_{j'} c_m c_{m'} ,
\end{equation}
an eight-state Potts model on the complete graph of 2\,880 sites, whose couplings are not independent: $H$ is a sum of $N$ rank-one terms (147 to 1\,334 calibration inputs per expert, according to the logs reread by the judge) plus the damping. One seeks a \emph{discrete} vector almost orthogonal to $N$ patterns $x_i$: this is the frame of the symmetric binary perceptron, for which Aubin, Perkins and Zdeborová conjecture a ``frozen'' landscape where local algorithms get stuck \cite{aubin2019perceptrons}. This connection is a frame, not a result: their patterns are independent Gaussians and their weights $\pm 1$. (iii) \emph{A Gibbs measure}, $\pi_T(q) \propto e^{-E(q)/T}$.

\begin{proposition}[The two dynamics preserve $\pi_T$]\label{t3mim:prop:gibbs}
(a) The heat bath on one coordinate, which redraws $q_j$ with probability $p_m \propto e^{-\Delta E_m/T}$ ($\Delta E_m$ of \cref{t3mim:lem:variation}), satisfies detailed balance for $\pi_T$; at $T \to 0$, it is cyclic coordinate descent. (b) In replica exchange at temperatures $T_1 < \dots < T_{n_t}$, accepting the exchange of the states of two copies $a, b$ with probability $\min\bigl(1, e^{(\beta_a - \beta_b)(E_a - E_b)}\bigr)$, $\beta = 1/T$, preserves the product law $\prod_a \pi_{T_a}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
(a) The drawing law is $\pi_T$ conditioned on the other coordinates: $P(q \to q') = \pi_T(q') / Z_j$, where $Z_j = \sum_m \pi_T(q \text{ with } j \leftarrow m)$ does not depend on the current level of $j$; so $\pi_T(q)\,P(q \to q') = \pi_T(q)\pi_T(q')/Z_j$, symmetric in $q, q'$. (b) The exchange multiplies the weight of the product law by $\dfrac{\pi_{T_a}(q_b)\,\pi_{T_b}(q_a)}{\pi_{T_a}(q_a)\,\pi_{T_b}(q_b)} = e^{-\beta_a E_b - \beta_b E_a + \beta_a E_a + \beta_b E_b} = e^{(\beta_a - \beta_b)(E_a - E_b)}$; the Metropolis rule on this ratio restores detailed balance.
\end{proof}
Simulated annealing is the same idea without exchange: a single copy whose $T$ decreases. Setting of step 1: 8 temperatures from $0.02$ to $1.0$ times the mean Babai error per weight, exchange at each sweep, 40 sweeps, then quenching at $T = 0$ of the best state visited; all copies start from Babai.

\paragraph{Why: the error moved into the kernel of the calibration.} Decompose the error in the eigenbasis $(\lambda_k, u_k)$ of $H_{\text{train}}$: $E = \sum_k e_k\,(u_k^\top H u_k)$, $e_k = \lVert \Delta W u_k \rVert^2$. An optimiser that minimises $\sum_k \lambda_k e_k$ at a roughly fixed budget of weight error \emph{moves} the error towards the directions of small $\lambda_k$. Yet the calibration $H$ has rank at most $N$, from 147 to 1\,334 for $n = 2\,880$; in its kernel it is zero and only the damping sees the error, whereas the evaluation places 40\,\% (Babai) to 47\,\% (descent) of its own there. On L8-E0 (32 rows), the evaluation error falls by 38\,\% in the first three groups of directions (0.00602 $\to$ 0.00373) and rises by 6\,\% in the other eleven (0.01225 $\to$ 0.01303) \mes~(\cref{fig:t3-mim-p5-spectre}). This is the mechanism of a model learnt on $N$ examples in dimension $n > N$: the empirical energy is flat where the true energy is not.

\subsection{The announced mechanism, and what the judge says of it}\label{t3mim:sec:p5-juge}

The lead looked for the cause of the non-transfer in the finiteness of the calibration sample and declared a test (addendum 3, commit \texttt{d958a46}): train on the calibration plus windows 0 to 4 of the evaluation, judge on windows 5 to 9; ``supported'' if the median gain of the descent on the test rises by at least 2 points and rises on at least 6 instances out of 8. It wrote: ``+5.9 to +7.3\,\% (\dots\ +2.6 points; \barre{supported threshold held})''. The judge (\fichier{genome/mimetisme/P5/JUGEMENT.md} §\,4) rereads the declaration: the two medians published side by side make $+1.41$ points, which falls into ``undecided''; the $+2.6$ is the median of the rises per instance, another quantity that the declaration did not fix. Then he isolates $N$ by a test declared beforehand (J5): \emph{remove} half of the calibration inputs, same source, same test. Dividing $N$ by 2.3 in median lowers the median gain by only 0.43 point, below the threshold of 0.5: \textbf{not supported}. What addendum 3 measured mixes ``more $N$'' and ``inputs taken from the evaluation file, neighbours of the test''. What is clear: $N$ matters for Babai itself (dividing $N$ by 2 costs it 6\,\% of evaluation error in median).

The judge's replica exchange reproduces the fall of the lead's objective to within 0.1 point on each instance, but not its evaluation gain: median 4.8\,\%, below its own descent (5.1\,\%), 0 / 8 above 10\,\% (the 12.3\,\% of L0-E16 becomes 6.3\,\%). At equal quality of objective, two exchanges land on states whose held-out error differs by 2 to 6 points: the ``$+1.4$ points above the descent'' is optimiser noise.

\paragraph{Verdict of the judge: open; the mechanism does not hold at the threshold.} The main number holds for cyclic descent, identically and on new weights; that of the exchange is reproduced only in class. Step 2 (real weights) is not authorised by the rule and has not run. The lever ``number of calibration inputs'' remains a hypothesis, to be measured with a fixed source and distant windows.

\section{P6: fidelity decided by call}\label{t3mim:sec:p6}

\paragraph{The idea.} The mixing weights of the router are a softmax over four scores, like the occupation numbers of four levels of a physical system; a low occupation weighs little in the output. The physical image (a Fermi surface) is an analogy: what it proposes is to serve in \emph{fast} mode (the compressed companion of lot B) the expert called with a small weight, instead of removing it. The idea of degrading according to importance is not new \cite{hobbit2024,lu2024notallexperts}, nor that of refining in mixed precision \cite{haidar2018mixed}; what P6 was to provide the project is the curve that the whole of lot B lacks, the KL as a function of the share $f$ of the misses served fast, of which only the ends were measured (0.0044 and 0.145).

\subsection{The model and its quantities}\label{t3mim:sec:p6-modele}

An access $a = (t, \ell, s)$ calls expert $x_a$ with weight $w_a$ ($\sum_s w = 1$). A \emph{stay} $\sigma$ of an expert in the cache runs from its entry (a miss) to its eviction; $A(\sigma)$ is the set of its accesses. The gate fixes the level of a stay at its entry: $\varphi(\sigma) = F$ (fast) if and only if $w_{a_0(\sigma)} < \theta$. A stay costs $\rho = 0.718$ exact expert if it is fast, 1 otherwise. The perturbed energy of the ``accept'' rule and the total energy are
\begin{equation}\label{t3mim:eq:p6-energie}
\mathcal E = \sum_\sigma \mathbb{1}[\varphi(\sigma) = F] \sum_{a \in A(\sigma)} w_a^2, \qquad \mathcal E_{\text{all}} = \sum_a w_a^2 .
\end{equation}
\begin{hypothese}[Small perturbation]\label{t3mim:hyp:energie}
The output deviation at access $a$ is $w_a\,\varepsilon_x$, with $\varepsilon_x$ an expert deviation of common variance; the total deviation variance is then proportional to the energy. This is not the KL.
\end{hypothese}
The bytes saved are $S = (1 - \rho)\,f$, $f$ being the share of fast stays; all-fast gives $S_{\text{all}} = 0.282$ and $\mathcal E = \mathcal E_{\text{all}}$. The quantity of the lead is the energy per byte saved, relative to that of all-fast:
\begin{equation}\label{t3mim:eq:p6-r}
r(\theta) = \frac{\mathcal E/\mathcal E_{\text{all}}}{S / S_{\text{all}}} = \frac{\mathcal E/\mathcal E_{\text{all}}}{f} .
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[The break by chance]\label{t3mim:prop:p6-hasard}
If $\varphi$ is drawn independently of the weights with $P(F) = f$, then $\E[\mathcal E] = f\,\mathcal E_{\text{all}}$ and $\E[r] = 1$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$\mathcal E = \sum_\sigma \varphi(\sigma)\,e(\sigma)$, with $e(\sigma) = \sum_{A(\sigma)} w^2$ independent of $\varphi(\sigma)$; so $\E[\mathcal E] = f \sum_\sigma e(\sigma) = f\,\mathcal E_{\text{all}}$.
\end{proof}
Measured: $1.0001 \pm 0.023$ over 50 draws and 7 traces \mes.

\begin{proposition}[The gate alone]\label{t3mim:prop:p6-porte}
Let $p(\theta) = P(w < \theta)$ and $\hat{\mathcal E}(\theta) = \E[w^2 ;\, w < \theta] / \E[w^2]$. For a count without carry-over (each access carries its own label) and a selection independent of the state of the cache, $r_0(\theta) = \hat{\mathcal E}(\theta)/p(\theta) = \E[w^2 \mid w < \theta]/\E[w^2] < 1$ as soon as the law of $w$ is not degenerate.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$\E[w^2;\, w<\theta] = p(\theta)\,\E[w^2 \mid w < \theta]$; since $w^2$ increases on $[0, 1]$, $\E[w^2 \mid w < \theta] < \E[w^2]$ if $P(w \ge \theta) > 0$ and $P(w<\theta)>0$.
\end{proof}
Measured: $r_0(0.20) = 0.414$, $r_0(0.25) = 0.614$ \mes. The lead concluded from it: \barre{``the misses are not correlated with the small weights; the gain of the gate comes from the law of $w$, not from the cache''}. That is false, says the judge (\fichier{genome/mimetisme/P6/JUGEMENT.md} §\,4.2): the misses are distributed as 19.4, 23.1, 26.8 and 30.6\,\% over ranks 0 to 3, and 29.6\,\% of the misses are below $\theta = 0.20$, against 24.9\,\% when the rank within the group is destroyed; the state of the cache gives the gate about 19\,\% more bytes saved at equal $\theta$ ($29.6/24.9 = 1.19$). The test of the lead (shuffling the vectors by layer) kept the rank: the correlation goes into $S$, not into $r$, which sees nothing of it.

\paragraph{Carry-over.} A ``fast'' label fixed at entry holds for the whole stay: $\mathcal E = \mathcal E_{\text{gate}} + \mathcal E_{\text{rest}}$. At $\theta = 0.20$ on the generations of the 120b, $\mathcal E_{\text{gate}}/\mathcal E_{\text{all}} = 0.0476$ and $\mathcal E_{\text{rest}}/\mathcal E_{\text{all}} = 0.1609$: the rest carries 77\,\% of the energy \mes. With $f = 0.0855/0.282 = 0.303$, $r = 0.2085/0.303 = 0.688$ \calc. The entry weight predicts the rest badly (correlation 0.34 and 0.25). The \emph{clairvoyant bound}, which would know the largest weight the expert will receive during its stay ($\varphi^\ast = F \iff \max_{A(\sigma)} w < \theta$), reaches $r^\ast = 0.185$: carry-over costs a factor 3.7. The rule ``promote at $\theta'$'' rereads the whole exact expert at the first hit of weight $\ge \theta'$, because the fast file is not nested in the exact one.

\paragraph{Variant B, prefill.} In a batch of $n_\beta$ tokens, each key $u = (\text{batch}, \ell, x)$ is read once. One reads in fast mode the $f$ keys of smallest score, with three scores: the maximum $m_u = \max_j w_j$ (rule A), the mass $M_u = \sum_j w_j$ (rule B), the energy $e_u = \sum_j w_j^2$ (rule C).

\begin{proposition}[The energy oracle]\label{t3mim:prop:p6-oracle}
At fixed $f$, rule C minimises $\mathcal E$ among all subsets of $f\,|U|$ keys; it therefore bounds A and B from below.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$\mathcal E = \sum_{u \in F} e_u$ is additive; the subset of $f|U|$ keys of smallest sum is that of the $f|U|$ smallest terms.
\end{proof}
The maximum is a poor substitute for $e_u$ because it ignores the number of calls ($m_u^2 \le e_u \le n_u m_u^2$); the mass $M_u = n_u \bar w_u$ counts them.

\paragraph{Decoding: open.} No rule passes 8\,\% on wiki ($7.78 \pm 0.38\,\%$ for ``accept''), none has $r > 0.90$ (\cref{t3mim:tab:p6}). Cumulated over the two traces, ``accept'' and ``promote at 0.40'' would pass, but the declared rule required each trace: verdict \textbf{open}, and the lead did not move its threshold. The judge adds that ``open'' comes from the choice of a fixed $\theta$ rather than from the mechanism: the weights pile up around 0.2, and $S$ goes from 2.2\,\% ($\theta = 0.15$) to 8.5\,\% (0.20) then 16\,\% (0.25); at exactly $S = 8\,\%$, trace by trace, $r$ is 0.669 and 0.573, both below 0.70 (exploratory, outside the threshold).

\paragraph{Variant B: holds, but could hardly fail.} At 8\,\% of the prefill bytes, $r_B = 0.039$ to $0.060$ (threshold 0.70) and $r_B/r_A = 0.61$ to $0.66$ on the four genres of the 120b, and $r_B$ from 0.030 to 0.074 on the 20b; random drawing gives $r = 1.00$ \mes. The threshold ``fails if $r_B \ge r_A$'' could hardly fail, since the maximum is a worse substitute for the energy than the sum whatever the law of the weights. At step 0, the mass says only that the energy of a batch concentrates in the often-called experts; it says nothing about the KL.

\paragraph{The curve KL$(f)$, set out as a hypothesis.} Step 0, that of the bytes--energy plane of \cref{fig:t3-mim-p6}, can only eliminate. Under the linear hypothesis $\mathrm{KL}(f) - \mathrm{KL}(0) = (\mathrm{KL}(1) - \mathrm{KL}(0))\,\mathcal E(f)/\mathcal E_{\text{all}}$, with the ends measured on the 20b (0.0044 and 0.1448), 8\,\% of the prefill bytes would cost 0.002 of KL and 14\,\% from 0.006 to 0.009. This is not a result: 96.5\,\% of the KL of the fast mode comes from routing flips, which are non-linear, and the convexity could go either way. Step 1, which would measure the points $(f, \mathrm{KL})$ on the 20b, has not been launched (lock on the card, 8.31\,GB to read); the judge asks that it keep the maximum, chance \emph{and} the energy oracle as comparisons at equal bytes: if the KL follows the energy, mass and oracle give the same KL.

\paragraph{Verdict of the judge.} Decoding of the 120b: \textbf{open}, reproduced. Variant B: ``holds'' at step 0, reproduced, but almost the oracle of its own measurement: nothing is said about quality. The gate saves 7.8 to 10.1\,\% of the bytes read in generation for 16 to 26\,\% of the energy of all-fast; only step 1 will say what the energy is worth.

\section{What could be wrong}\label{t3mim:sec:faux}

\begin{faux}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Two generation traces for the 120b, and they are degenerate.} P1, P2, P4 and P6 judge the generation on the same two traces of 256 tokens; half of prose-en is made of tokens produced after the end of the message, and wiki is a raw completion that repeats itself. The judges of P1 and P4 said so; those of P2 and P6 did not raise it, and their generation numbers carry the same reserve.
\item \textbf{The simulated hit rates exceed those of the engine} (0.74 against 0.59 on prose-en): every simulated cache gain (P1, P2, P4, P6) is an upper bound before the ABBA of the engine. The expert sizes are equal in it, the warm RAM absent.
\item \textbf{P3 rests only on its judgements}: a single budget carries the ``holds'', a single reading text, a single online pass; the second draw, in progress elsewhere, may confirm it as well as contradict it, and this chapter does not know it.
\item \textbf{The simulator of P4 is not validated} (T3 missed): its gains are orders of magnitude with an unknown sign of error.
\item \textbf{P5 is measured on synthetic} Gaussian weights, on 64 rows and the sole input $H$ of gate and up; the real weights have giant columns correlated with $H$, and the Babai--optimum gap may be larger or smaller there. No claim about the quality of the text.
\item \textbf{P6 is measured in energy}, not in KL; and its ``holds'' at prefill could hardly fail.
\item \textbf{The proposition on the inclusive cache} (\cref{t3mim:prop:inclusif}) is set out by the writing under a hypothesis on the rule of the engine (refreshing of the RAM at each access) read in the code by the judge, not executed; it explains the equality measured by the judge, it does not replace it.
\item \textbf{The sources of the leads}: the sorting reopened no external source; the writing reread on 2026-10-29 the records of those this chapter cites (Crossref, arXiv, USENIX, RFC), not the papers. The scouts cited by number (``life \#1'') are known here only through their reports.
\item \textbf{The ranking of the sorting is not measured}: ``gain $\times$ novelty $\times$ feasibility'' is a judgement of order; two leads ruled out (reverse water-filling by usage, BOLA) might be worth more than P4 or P5 once put in numbers.
\item \textbf{The figures are outside the common style}: drawn by each lead, with their own titles (among them that of \cref{fig:t3-mim-p1-blocs}, which the judge corrects), their own number formatting and a panel title that runs over; they are included as they are, and the numbers read on them have been checked against the judgements, not redrawn.
\end{itemize}
\end{faux}
