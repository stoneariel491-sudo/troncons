\chapter{The judge of the construction}\label{t3jug:ch}

\begin{origine}
\textbf{At the start}, two lots that call themselves finished, and figures produced by their authors alone, on the windows where their mechanisms were tuned. Lot A publishes a lossless \texttt{.trc} v1.2 format (the sign of a zero folded, \SI{1.80}{\Go} fewer on the 120b), logits identical to the bit on R1, and a J1 of the 120b 1.45 times faster in v1.2, which it did not itself attribute to the format (\cref{t3cod:ch}); the placement judge has since attributed it to the v1.2 format, through the pattern of its read requests, specific to this disk (reserve R1, \cref{t3cod:sec:motif} and \cref{t3jug:sec:r1}). Lot B publishes the synonymous genome on the 768 experts of the 20b, the rule B2 that keeps $\lambda = 0$ narrowly, a fast mode in the engine and two speed gains, $\times 1.24$ and $\times 2.00$ (\cref{t3sno:ch}). The SOCLE requires the work to be reread by a judge that did not do it.
\textbf{This chapter establishes}, by a judge's own paths (a decoder of the format written from its description alone, a GGUF reader of its own, a KL computed by its code, ten windows of text that the lots never saw, a speed session with three files), what holds, what falls, and what is true but wrongly attributed; and it poses in full the five derivations on which its verdicts rest.
\textbf{What remains} is the decision on the mixed miss policy, another corpus, and the exact variable of the read pattern that makes the speed of this disk (\cref{t3jug:sec:reste}).
Sources: \fichier{genome/JUGEMENT-construction.md}, \fichier{genome/construction-juge-pour-le-memoire.md}, \fichier{genome/JUGEMENT-placement.md}, \fichier{mesures/genome/construction/juge/} (thresholds: \fichier{00-seuils-declares.md}) and \fichier{outils/genome/construction/juge/}.
\end{origine}

\begin{enbref}
A judge redoes. It does not reread the others' computations: it makes other ones, by paths that share with those of the lots only what they must share, the description of the format and the files judged. The judge of the construction wrote neither the decision on the v1.2 format, nor the coder, nor the fast companion, nor the engine. It declared its thresholds on 22 October 2026 around 4 a.m., when none of its tools had yet read one byte of a model. Its verdict fits in two sentences. The construction delivers what it promised \emph{in bytes and in quality}: the zero-synonym codon is lossless and exact to the bit on two models and on new text; the synonymous genome $\lambda = 0$ beats the frontier and $\lambda = 1$ on this new text, more clearly than on the text where it had been chosen. It does not deliver the speed gain announced for the fast mode: the two published ratios are reproduced, but they mostly measured the way this disk reads the old format. This chapter shows how the judge separates it, and why it can be believed.
\end{enbref}

\section{The \texttt{.trc} v1.2 through the judge's decoder}\label{t3jug:sec:jt}

\subsection{Vectorised rANS decoding is normative decoding}\label{t3jug:sec:vectorise}

An rANS stream of the format is cut into independent tiles of 32 lanes \parencite{duda2013ans,giesen2014interleaved}. Normative decoding (specification § 4.9.2) goes through the macro-steps $m$ of a tile, then its sub-steps $k$, then its lanes $l = 0, \dots, 31$ \emph{in order}. In Python, a code matrix of $\num{2880} \times \num{2880}$ weights would require \num{4147200} sequential steps; the judge does them in \num{1024} iterations (256 macro-steps $\times$ 4 sub-steps), on an array of $T \times 32$ states at once. It must then be proved that this form is normative decoding.

\paragraph{Notation.} A tile has 32 lanes of states $x_l \in [L, 2^{32})$ and a pointer $p$ into the shared array \texttt{mots}. A step of lane $l$ computes, with the table $c$ of the context of its word: $\text{slot} = x_l \bmod M$; $s = \mathrm{sym}_c(\text{slot})$; $y_l = f_c(s)\,\lfloor x_l / M\rfloor + \text{slot} - \mathrm{cum}_c(s)$; if $y_l < L$, $x_l \leftarrow 2^{16} y_l + \texttt{mots}[p]$ and $p \leftarrow p + 1$, otherwise $x_l \leftarrow y_l$. The format fixes $M = 2^{12}$ and $L = 2^{16}$ (Volume~II, chapter on the format).

\begin{lemme}[J1: the lanes are independent within a sub-step]\label{t3jug:lem:j1}
At sub-step $(m, k)$, $y_l$ depends only on $x_l$ and on the table of the context of word $32m + l$.
\end{lemme}
\begin{proof}
The only inputs of the computation of $y_l$ are $x_l$, the slot (a function of $x_l$) and the table; no other lane enters it, and the pointer $p$ intervenes only afterwards, in the renormalisation.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[J2: the assignment of the words is a prefix sum]\label{t3jug:prop:j2}
Let $r_l = [y_l < L]$ and $R_l = \sum_{l' \le l} r_{l'}$. Sequential processing $l = 0, \dots, 31$ gives every lane $l$ such that $r_l = 1$ the word $\texttt{mots}[p_0 + R_l - 1]$, where $p_0$ is the pointer at the start of the sub-step, and leaves the pointer at $p_0 + R_{31}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By induction on $l$. Before lane $l$, the pointer is $p_0 + R_{l-1}$ (with $R_{-1} = 0$): each of the preceding lanes that renormalised advanced it by one. If $r_l = 1$, the lane reads $\texttt{mots}[p_0 + R_{l-1}] = \texttt{mots}[p_0 + R_l - 1]$ and brings the pointer to $p_0 + R_l$; otherwise it stays at $p_0 + R_{l-1} = p_0 + R_l$. At the end, it is $p_0 + R_{31}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remarque}[A single word per lane and per sub-step]
The proposition assumes that a lane that renormalises reads only one word. This is true: before the step, $x_l \ge L = 2^{16}$, so $y_l \ge \lfloor x_l / 2^{12}\rfloor \ge 2^{4}$ since $f_c(s) \ge 1$ and $\text{slot} - \mathrm{cum}_c(s) \ge 0$; if $y_l < L$, the new state $2^{16} y_l + w \ge 2^{20} \ge L$. Volume~II poses the same argument in the lemma of the vectorised decoder of trial J1.
\end{remarque}

By lemma J1, all the $y_l$ of a sub-step are computed before any write; by proposition J2, the new states too, by the prefix sum \texttt{cumsum(renouvelle)} along the lane axis. The tiles being independent (each starts from its beginning and its 32 states), the decoder processes the $T \times 32$ lanes of all the tiles of a stream at each sub-step. The end of tile (every lane back on $L$) is checked at the end. A code matrix is thus decoded in about \SI{0.5}{\second}, a whole expert in \SIrange{1.8}{2.1}{\second}, checks included \mes.

\subsection{J-T1: 96 drawn experts, decoded by the judge}\label{t3jug:sec:jt1}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{96 experts out of 96 hold the eight checks} \mes.
\item \textbf{The control} (answer known in advance): the 32 experts of the 20b and the first 8 drawn from the 120b, decoded in \emph{v1.0}, return the GGUF as it is (40 out of 40); and for each, the nibbles that change between the v1.0 decoded and the v1.2 decoded are exactly the $-0$ of the GGUF (L19 E109 of the 120b: \num{1746601} changed, \num{1746601} $-0$) \mes.

\subsection{What the v1.0 \texorpdfstring{$\to$}{->} v1.2 control proves}\label{t3jug:sec:temoin}

Let $D_0(x)$ and $D_2(x)$ be the decoded nibbles of expert $x$ in v1.0 and v1.2, $G(x)$ those of the GGUF, and $\kappa(g) = g$ if $g \ne 8$, $0$ otherwise (code 8 is the $-0$ of E2M1). The control measures $D_0(x) = G(x)$, and check (b) measures $D_2(x) = \kappa(G(x))$. Then
\[
\{ i : D_0(x)_i \ne D_2(x)_i \} = \{ i : G(x)_i \ne \kappa(G(x)_i) \} = \{ i : G(x)_i = 8 \}:
\]
the set of changed nibbles is \emph{exactly} that of the $-0$, which the log checks expert by expert. This is the measured form of the sentence ``v1.2 changes only the sign of the zeros'', and, with (d), of ``v1.2 is lossless in value''.

\paragraph{Verdict J-T: holds.} The \texttt{.trc} v1.2 is exactly what the format describes, and each drawn expert is the expert of the GGUF to the bit, up to the sign of the zeros.

\section{J-K: the KL of the synonymous genome outside R1}\label{t3jug:sec:kl}

\subsection{The judge's KL, and its control}\label{t3jug:sec:kljuge}

For each scored position, the judge computes
\[
\mathrm{KL}(P \Vert Q) = \sum_{i \,:\, \log p_i > -16} p_i\,(\log p_i - \log q_i),
\]
$P$ and $Q$ decoded from their 16-bit coding, without renormalisation: this is the convention of lot R (Volume~II, the reference and the judge), where a file against itself returns 0 \parencite{kullback1951}. \textbf{Control}: on a pair published by lot B (R1, $\lambda = 0$), its KL is \num{0.131752831} against \num{0.131752832} for \texttt{juge kl} ($1.1 \cdot 10^{-8}$ relative, threshold $10^{-6}$); \textbf{break}: $Q$ shifted by one position, \num{1.634} \mes. R1 recomputed by its code from the files of lot B: frontier \num{0.26399}, $\lambda = 1$ \num{0.15216}, $\lambda = 0$ \num{0.13175}; paired $-\num{0.11183} \pm \num{0.02962}$, $-\num{0.02040} \pm \num{0.00974}$ ($\bar d + 2\sigma = -\num{0.00092}$), $-\num{0.13224} \pm \num{0.02343}$: the figures of lot B are given again to four digits and more \mes.

\subsection{The uncertainty of a mean KL: by positions or by windows}\label{t3jug:sec:sigma}

The 255 scored positions of a window share their past, hence their routing flips: they are not independent. Two standard deviations of the mean stand against each other:
\[
\sigma_f = \frac{s(\bar K_1, \dots, \bar K_{10})}{\sqrt{10}},
\qquad
\sigma_n = \frac{s(K_1, \dots, K_{\num{2550}})}{\sqrt{\num{2550}}},
\]
the first on the ten window means, the second on the positions as if they were independent. Their ratio defines the \emph{design effect} and the number of effective positions:
\[
\mathrm{deff} = \left(\frac{\sigma_f}{\sigma_n}\right)^2,\qquad n_{\text{eff}} = \frac{n}{\mathrm{deff}} .
\]

\begin{proposition}[The design effect of a cluster sample]\label{t3jug:prop:deff}
Let there be $n$ clusters (the windows) of $m$ positions, each position of variance $s^2$, two positions of the same cluster with correlation $\rho$, and two positions of different clusters independent. Then the variance of the overall mean is $\dfrac{s^2}{nm}\,\bigl(1 + (m - 1)\rho\bigr)$, and $\mathrm{deff} = 1 + (m - 1)\rho$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The mean of a cluster, $\bar K_g = \frac1m \sum_{i} K_{gi}$, has variance $\frac{1}{m^2}\bigl(m s^2 + m(m-1)\rho s^2\bigr) = \frac{s^2}{m}\bigl(1 + (m-1)\rho\bigr)$: $m$ variances and $m(m-1)$ covariances. The clusters being independent, the mean of the $n$ means has $n$ times less variance. The naive variance, which ignores the covariances, is $s^2/(nm)$; the quotient is $1 + (m-1)\rho$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{On R1, frontier.} $\sigma_f = \num{0.0376}$, $\sigma_n = \num{0.0066}$: $\mathrm{deff} \approx 32$ and $n_{\text{eff}} \approx 79$ \calc. A window of 255 positions is therefore worth about eight independent positions ($255/32$), and by the proposition, the mean correlation of two positions of the same window is $\rho \approx (32 - 1)/254 \approx \num{0.12}$ \calc. \Cref{fig:t3-jcons-fenetres} shows where it comes from: some windows are harder than others for all the variants at once (the frontier ranges from \num{0.136} to \num{0.459} from one window of R1 to another), and the exact mode stays far below all of them (from \num{0.0002} to \num{0.0219}).

\subsection{Redoing a result at two standard deviations}\label{t3jug:sec:refaire}

Why did the judge predict ``equal''? Take a paired difference measured at $z = \bar d/\sigma$ standard deviations, and suppose that the true difference is exactly $\bar d$, and that the second series of ten windows has the same $\sigma$. The new estimator roughly follows $\mathcal{N}(\bar d, \sigma^2)$; with $\bar d = -z\sigma$, the probability that it passes the threshold $\bar d' + 2\sigma < 0$ again is
\[
P\bigl(\bar d' < -2\sigma\bigr) = \Phi\!\left(\frac{-2\sigma + z\sigma}{\sigma}\right) = \Phi(z - 2).
\]
For $z = \num{2.09}$, $\Phi(\num{0.09}) \approx \num{0.54}$ \calc: barely more than one chance in two. And if the true difference is smaller than $\bar d$, which is likely for a result kept \emph{because it} passed the threshold, the probability falls. The prediction followed this computation; the measurement refuted it, $z = \num{3.62}$ on the new windows. The judge did not change its prediction afterwards: it was declared, and the measurement decided.

\section{J-V: speed, and what it separates}\label{t3jug:sec:vitesse}

\subsection{The trap}\label{t3jug:sec:piege}

Each of the two lots compared a new file with an old file: lot A the v1.2, written on 17 October, with the original v1.0, written on the 15th; lot B its companions, written on the 17th and the 22nd, with the same original v1.0. Lot A saw the trap and said so: two v1.0 files identical to the bit were read at \num{1.74} and \SI{1.34}{\Go\per\second}. Lot B did not see it. If two files of the same model are read at different speeds for a reason that is not their mechanism, a speed ratio between them does not measure the mechanism.

\subsection{The three-file session}\label{t3jug:sec:plan}

The judge puts the three files of each model into a single session: A the original v1.0, B the v1.2 (same writer as the companions, written like them after the 15th), R the fast companion, in the order
\[
\text{A B R R B A A B R R B A} .
\]

\begin{proposition}[The plan balances a linear drift]\label{t3jug:prop:plan}
If the speed of a pass of rank $i$ of file X is $v_X\,(1 + a + b\,i)$, the ratio of the means of the four passes of two files is $v_X / v_Y$, without a drift term. A quadratic drift, for its part, is not balanced.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The ranks are A $\{1, 6, 7, 12\}$, B $\{2, 5, 8, 11\}$, R $\{3, 4, 9, 10\}$; their sums are all 26, their means $\bar\imath = \num{6.5}$. The mean of the four passes of X is $v_X\,(1 + a + b\,\bar\imath) = v_X\,(1 + a + 6.5\,b)$, the same factor for the three files, which disappears from the ratio. The sums of the squares of the ranks are $1 + 36 + 49 + 144 = 230$, $4 + 25 + 64 + 121 = 214$ and $9 + 16 + 81 + 100 = 206$: a term in $c\,i^2$ would leave a bias.
\end{proof}
The rule reads \emph{medians} of four passes, that is the mean of the two in the middle. Under a monotone drift that dominates the noise, the middle passes are those of ranks 6 and 7 for A, 5 and 8 for B, 4 and 9 for R, whose mean is again \num{6.5}: the median is balanced in this case too \calc.

\paragraph{The decomposition.} With three files from the same session,
\[
r(R/A) = r(R/B) \cdot r(B/A) .
\]
$r(B/A)$ measures what lot A measured, $r(R/A)$ what lot B measured, and $r(R/B)$ what the fast mode is worth against an exact file that the disk stage reads at the same speed. The ceiling that a smaller format can return, with the disk limiting, is the ratio of the bytes read per step ($o_A / o_B = 1/\num{0.9671} = \num{1.034}$ for v1.2); any $r(B/A)$ beyond it comes from something other than the bytes. The disk stage alone (\texttt{troncons disque --n 1500}, the instrument of lot A) runs first, A B R R B A, and says which of the factors is not the mechanism.

\paragraph{The threshold.} With $\sigma = \max(\sigma_A, \sigma_B, \sigma_R)$, relative standard deviations of the four passes, a published gain $g$ is a gain of the fast mode if $r(R/B) \ge g\,(1 - 2\sigma)$; $(1 - 2\sigma)$ is the lower bound of a ratio of medians at two standard deviations of its noise. The conditions: mains power at each pass; reading of the other streams before and after each pass (more than \SI{5}{\percent} of a core or more than \SI{8}{\Mo} read in \SI{3}{\second}: ``under load'', the pass does not conclude); start on two empty readings one minute apart; lock held. \textbf{None of the 36 passes was under load} \mes.

\subsection{What makes the disk stage: reserve R1}\label{t3jug:sec:r1}

The judge of the construction had first attributed the gap of the disk stage to the physical state of the files, after a reading of the disk placement stream: \barre{it is the state of the files, not the format (+3.4 \% at most)}. \begin{itemize}

\section{What the judge's method teaches}\label{t3jug:sec:recettes}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{A three-file session separates the format, the mode and the file}: $r(R/A) = r(R/B)\cdot r(B/A)$, and the disk stage measured in the same session says which of the factors is not the mechanism. One hour per model; it brought down two figures.
\item \textbf{Before attributing a speed gain to a mechanism, measure the disk stage of each file in the same session}: one minute.
\item \textbf{A judge's decoder written from the description alone, in another language, vectorised by prefix sum}: two seconds per expert, right to the bit at the first attempt on the decoding.
\item \textbf{New windows drawn far from the region already seen, merged into a single file} that all the tools read without change.
\item \textbf{Do not compare a 99.9th percentile of \num{2550} positions from one variant to another}: it rests on two or three positions.
\item \textbf{The text of a lock counts}: the judge's first lock said ``pas une séance de vitesse'' (\emph{not a speed session}), and the leads stream, which looked for the word ``vitesse'' (\emph{speed}), paused for nothing.
\item \textbf{Stopping a bash loop that launches a native program under Windows does not kill the program}: the f64 pass in progress finished on its own, and its complete output was kept.
\end{itemize}

\section{What remains}\label{t3jug:sec:reste}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The mixed miss policy} is to be taken up with $r(R/B)$: on the 120b, $\times 1.28$ in generation for a KL of \num{0.14} (proxy); on the 20b, nothing in generation. Simulate it on the traces before building it.
\item \textbf{The speed lever that the construction found is not a mechanism of the genome}: it is the pattern of read requests of the format on this disk. The exact variable remains to be isolated, at the disk stage, by patterns declared beforehand (placement judge, § 12).
\item \textbf{Another corpus} (code, dialogue) for the KL of the synonymous genome, and the synonymous mutation of the 120b.
\item The judge's heavy files (about \SI{13}{\Go} of \texttt{.kld} outside the repository) can be regenerated by the committed tools; they can be erased whenever Stone wishes.
\end{enumerate}

\section{What could be wrong}\label{t3jug:sec:faux}

\begin{faux}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{One session per model, a single morning} (22 October, from 6:17 to 7:11 for the engine). The ratios $r(R/B)$ rest on the fact, measured in the same session, that B and R are read at the same speed at the disk stage (R/B $= \num{0.956}$ and \num{1.009}, below the separation threshold of \SI{11.8}{\percent}); if this were no longer true on another day, the session would have to be redone, not the figure reused.
\item \textbf{The settings are not all those of lot B}: on the 120b, those of J1 for the three files; the judge's $r(R/A)$ (\num{1.85}) differs from that of the lot (2.00) for this reason at least. What judges the attribution is $r(R/B)$, measured with the same settings for R and B.
\item \textbf{The new windows are from wiki.test}, like R1 and the calibration: another text, not another kind of text.
\item \textbf{The judge's decoder reads the same description as the authors of the coder.} A shared misreading would not be seen by comparing the decoded bytes… except that it would have been: the GGUF is read by a reader with nothing of the \texttt{.trc} format, and the fingerprints are SHA-256 computed on the judge's side \parencite{fips180-4}. What is not covered: the parts of the format that neither v1.0 nor v1.2 exercise (levels, raw, columns).
\item \textbf{96 experts out of \num{5376} (\SI{1.8}{\percent})} by the judge's path. The probability that a defect touching at least \SI{5}{\percent} of the experts of a model escapes 32 draws is $0.95^{32} \approx \SI{19}{\percent}$ (20b), and at 64 draws $0.95^{64} \approx \SI{3.75}{\percent}$ (120b) \calc{} (the source writes 3.7\,\%, a rounding too low). The verifier of lot A, for its part, covers \SI{100}{\percent} of the experts; the draw judges this verifier, it does not replace it.
\item \textbf{The judge's KL shares the convention of lot R} (cut at $\log p > -16$, without renormalisation): independent of the code, not of the convention. The paired differences hardly depend on it.
\item \textbf{The judge's predictions were wrong twice} (B2 ``equal'', measured \num{3.6}\,$\sigma$; $r(R/B)$ of the 120b $\approx 1.38$, measured \num{1.28}); they were declared beforehand, and the measurement decided.
\item \textbf{$\lambda = 1$ through the substituted reference was not run} on the new windows; B2 is judged there only by the engine, the frontier only by the substituted reference.
\item \textbf{The engine and \texttt{genome-capture}} that the judge ran are built from the same code as those of the lots: it judges the files and the claims, not a second engine. The second path for the logits remains the f64 reference of lot R.
\item \textbf{Reserve R1 itself}: the read pattern is established on this disk, by the placement judge, at the disk stage; the exact variable is not isolated, and another reading could replace it again. The verdicts of this chapter depend only on what the disk stage measures in the session, not on its explanation.
\end{itemize}
\end{faux}
