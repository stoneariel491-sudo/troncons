\begin{enbref}
A synonymous mutation changes a codon without changing the protein (\cref{t3int:sec:genome}). The scout looked, in the weights of gpt-oss, for the equivalent of this redundancy, keeping the analogy for what it is: an image that guides, not an argument. It found two. The first is in the format itself: MXFP4 has sixteen codes for fifteen values, because $+0$ and $-0$ are the same number; the sign of a zero is a piece of information that the computation never reads, like a part of the genome that codes for nothing, and removing it saves 3.6\,\% of the bytes without changing a single bit of the logits. The second is in the way of being wrong: a weight error in a direction that the real inputs of the expert do not excite is invisible at its output. Choosing the symbols of the base so as to store the error there costs the decoder nothing and divides the output error by three to four.
\end{enbref}

\paragraph{Why the sign of a zero is random} \calc. An MXFP4 quantiser rounds to nearest. An original weight of small magnitude becomes $\pm0$ according to its own sign, and the signs of the small weights are equiprobable. The format keeps this sign because E2M1 codes the sign separately~\cite{ocpmx2023}; the computation never sees it, since $K[8] = -0 = K[0]$. It is information inherited from the quantisation, the sign of a BF16 weight that no longer exists, without any effect on the value.

The consequence is a warning that the whole round verified at its own expense. A rate allocation that served only the heavy blocks would ``beat'' the frontier in the pooled metric while destroying 85\,\% of the weights. \textbf{The metric of the weights is not a judge.} Hence the judge of \cref{t3ecl:sec:mutation} (the output error of the experts on held-out inputs), then that of \cref{t3ecl:sec:kl} (the end-to-end KL).

\subsection{The bound of a memoryless coder}

The third look is the rate--distortion bound of \cref{t3que:sec:frontiere}: at the error of the frontier, an ideal coder that looks only at the weights would read at most 2.168 bits, that is 0.476 bit less; this gain is in the shape of the quantiser, not in a structure of the weights. The three looks say the same thing: seen by itself, an MXFP4 weight is noise around its scale. The two mechanisms that follow therefore add no structure to the weights: the first removes what the computation does not read, the second moves the error to where the inputs do not look.

\section{Mechanism 1: the zero-synonym codon}\label{t3ecl:sec:zero}

\subsection{The mechanism}

Before entropy coding, each nibble 8 ($-0$) becomes 0 ($+0$). The decoder does not change: it returns the canonical code, and the table of the stream no longer has a frequency for 8 (in the byte alphabet of A\_ctx, the 256 symbols become 225 possible ones, $15 \times 15$). The cost of transcription is zero. The analogy is that of non-coding DNA: a part of the file, 0.128 bit per weight, carries a sign that nobody reads.

\subsection{Why the products do not change}

\begin{proposition}[Bit exactness of a dot product after $-0 \to +0$]\label{t3ecl:prop:zero}
Let $y = \mathrm{fl}(\cdots\mathrm{fl}(\mathrm{fl}(t_1 + t_2) + t_3)\cdots + t_n)$ be an IEEE~754 sum with round-to-nearest~\cite{ieee754}, in a fixed order, of terms $t_j = \mathrm{fl}(w_j x_j)$ with finite $x_j$, and $w'$ equal to $w$ except $w'_j = +0$ where $w_j = -0$. If at least one term $t_j$ is non-zero, then $y' = y$ to the bit. If all are zero, $y, y' \in \{+0, -0\}$ and $y = y'$ in value.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
We have $t'_j = t_j$ if $w_j \ne -0$, and $t_j, t'_j \in \{+0, -0\}$ otherwise, since $(\pm0)\cdot x_j = \pm0$ for finite $x_j$. Let $S_k$ and $S'_k$ be the partial sums. Induction hypothesis: $S_k = S'_k$ to the bit, or else $S_k, S'_k \in \{\pm0\}$. It holds for $k = 1$. At step $k+1$, three cases. (i) If $t_{k+1} = t'_{k+1}$ is non-zero: when $S_k = S'_k$, the operands are equal and so is the sum; when $S_k, S'_k \in \{\pm0\}$, $\mathrm{fl}(\pm0 + t) = t$ exactly for $t \ne 0$, so $S_{k+1} = S'_{k+1} = t_{k+1}$. (ii) If $t_{k+1}, t'_{k+1} \in \{\pm0\}$ and $S_k = S'_k \ne 0$: $\mathrm{fl}(S \pm 0) = S$ on both sides. (iii) If both partial sums and both terms are zero, both sums stay in $\{\pm0\}$: only the sign of a zero can differ, since $(+0) + (-0) = +0$ but $(-0) + (-0) = -0$. At the first non-zero term, (i) joins the two sequences again, and (ii) keeps them equal afterwards. If no term is non-zero, only (iii) applies.
\end{proof}

In an integer kernel (E2M1 values doubled into signed integers), $-0$ and $+0$ are already the same integer. The case of a product of 2\,880 terms all zero does not occur in gpt-oss, where no block is zero; if it did, only the sign of an output zero would change, then disappear at the non-zero bias of the expert. For the f64 reference, the same induction applies to each of its eight accumulators, then to their tree reduction.

The proposition leaves open the sign of a zero partial sum. \emph{Proposition A1} of \cref{t3cod:ch} closes it for the GEMV kernel of the engine: an accumulator that starts from $+0$ and advances by FMA with round-to-nearest is never $-0$ (an exact zero sum of two terms that are not both $-0$ rounds to $+0$), so that a step on a zero code is the same in both computations, and bit equality holds at each step, even for an entirely zero sum. The proposition of this chapter suffices for the reference; A1 is what the engine needs.

\subsection{The same codon in the base}

In the base (sign and class), a zero keeps its sign: the class $\{0,\,0.5\}$ is rebuilt as $\pm0.237\,s$. On the canonical code, this sign is no longer stored ($-0.0476$ bit, \cref{t3ecl:tab:zero}); the decoder can \emph{draw it from a hash of the position} (\texttt{splitmix64} of $rC + c$) without reading a bit. The error on the weights is the same to the weight, since $|0 - (\pm0.237\,s)|$ does not depend on the sign. A Weyl sequence as hash fails (neighbouring rows almost equal); \texttt{splitmix64} holds (correlation 0.01, at the noise level). The \emph{output} error of this variant has not been measured: its pass was stopped after 4 experts (\fichier{passes/frontiere0-ARRETEE-4-experts.log}). It remains ``equal in weight error at $-0.048$ bit, not judged on the output''.

\section{Mechanism 2: the synonymous mutation}\label{t3ecl:sec:mutation}

\subsection{Life, and what is transposed from it}

The genetic code is not an arbitrary function from codons to amino acids: among a million possible codes, the natural code is one of the best at making the probable errors fall on similar amino acids~\cite{freeland1998}. The neuron, for its part, codes a continuous signal in spikes by carrying its error over to what follows, in the manner of a sigma-delta modulator~\cite{yoon2017sigmadelta}. Transposed, as an analogy: the ``phenotype'' of an expert matrix is its output $Wx$ on the inputs the model really gives it; a weight error in a direction that these inputs do not excite is a synonymous mutation. The mechanism chooses the symbols of the base so that the error goes there.

\subsection{The derivation}

\paragraph{The output error.} For a matrix $W$ of $R$ rows and $C$ columns, its inputs $x$ and the router weight $g$ of the expert, the output error of a reconstruction $\hat W$ is, by~\eqref{t3que:eq:sortie},
\begin{equation}\label{t3ecl:eq:erreur}
\E\bigl[g^2 \|(W - \hat W) x\|^2\bigr] = \E[g^2]\ \mathrm{tr}\bigl((W - \hat W)\, H\, (W - \hat W)^\top\bigr), \qquad H = \frac{\E[g^2 x x^\top]}{\E[g^2]} .
\end{equation}
The trace is a sum over the rows: the rows of $W$ are treated independently. For a row $w$, we seek $\min_q (w - q)^\top H (w - q)$, $q$ ranging over the reconstructions allowed column by column, $q_j = \pm c_k\, s_{b(j)}$, $c_k$ one of the four centres of the base and $s_{b(j)}$ the E8M0 scale of the block of column $j$.

\begin{lemme}[Optimal error feedback]\label{t3ecl:lem:obs}
Let $w$ be the current row (error already carried over included), $j$ a column and $F$ the set of columns still free, $j \in F$, $H_F$ the submatrix of $H$ on $F$. If column $j$ is fixed to $q_j$ and the other columns of $F$ are left to adjust, the minimal increase of the squared error $\delta^\top H_F \delta$ is
\begin{equation}\label{t3ecl:eq:obs}
\frac{(w_j - q_j)^2}{[H_F^{-1}]_{jj}}, \qquad \text{reached by } \delta = -\frac{w_j - q_j}{[H_F^{-1}]_{jj}}\ H_F^{-1} e_j ,
\end{equation}
where $\delta$ is the modification of $w$ on $F$; on the free columns other than $j$, the correction is $\delta_{F\setminus j} = -(w_j - q_j)\,[H_F^{-1}]_{F\setminus j,\,j} / [H_F^{-1}]_{jj}$.
\end{lemme}

\begin{proof}
We minimise $\delta^\top H_F \delta$ under the constraint $e_j^\top (w + \delta) = q_j$, that is $e_j^\top \delta = q_j - w_j$. The Lagrangian is $L(\delta, \mu) = \delta^\top H_F \delta + \mu\,(e_j^\top \delta - (q_j - w_j))$. Its gradient in $\delta$, $2 H_F \delta + \mu e_j$, vanishes at $\delta = -\frac{\mu}{2} H_F^{-1} e_j$. The constraint gives $-\frac{\mu}{2}\,[H_F^{-1}]_{jj} = q_j - w_j$, that is $\frac{\mu}{2} = -\frac{q_j - w_j}{[H_F^{-1}]_{jj}}$, hence $\delta = \frac{q_j - w_j}{[H_F^{-1}]_{jj}} H_F^{-1} e_j$, which is the stated expression. The increase is $\delta^\top H_F \delta = \frac{(q_j - w_j)^2}{[H_F^{-1}]_{jj}^2}\, e_j^\top H_F^{-1} H_F H_F^{-1} e_j = \frac{(q_j - w_j)^2}{[H_F^{-1}]_{jj}}$. $H_F$ being positive definite, it is a minimum. This is the Optimal Brain Surgeon lemma~\cite{hassibi1992obs}, applied to quantisation by OBC and GPTQ~\cite{frantar2022obc,frantar2022gptq}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{The sequential rule, through a single factor.} In the order of the columns $1, \ldots, C$, the free set at step $j$ is $F_j = \{j, \ldots, C\}$. The quantities $[H_{F_j}^{-1}]_{jj}$ and $[H_{F_j}^{-1}]_{F_j j}$ are read in the upper Cholesky factor $U$ of $H^{-1}$ ($U^\top U = H^{-1}$): $[H_{F_j}^{-1}]_{jj} = U_{jj}^2$, and the correction of the following columns is $-\frac{w_j - q_j}{U_{jj}}\, U_{j, F_j}$ (GPTQ). The factor is computed by a single factorisation.

\begin{lemme}[The Cholesky factor of $H^{-1}$ by reversal]\label{t3ecl:lem:chol}
Let $P$ be the permutation that reverses the order of the columns. If $PHP = LL^\top$, $L$ lower triangular with positive diagonal, then $U = P L^{-1} P$ is upper triangular with positive diagonal and $U^\top U = H^{-1}$: it is the upper Cholesky factor of $H^{-1}$.
\end{lemme}

\begin{proof}
$P = P^\top = P^{-1}$. From $PHP = LL^\top$ comes $H = (PLP)(PLP)^\top = VV^\top$ with $V = PLP$, \emph{upper} triangular (reversing the order of the rows and columns of a lower triangular matrix makes it upper triangular). Then $H^{-1} = V^{-\top} V^{-1} = U^\top U$ with $U = V^{-1} = P L^{-1} P$, upper triangular as the inverse of an upper triangular matrix, with diagonal $1/L_{C+1-i,\,C+1-i} > 0$. The Cholesky factor with positive diagonal is unique: it is $U$.
\end{proof}

The cost is $n^3/3$ (Cholesky) plus $n^3/3$ (triangular inverse), instead of $n^3$ (inverse) plus $n^3/3$ \calc; the tool checked it against the direct computation, relative gap $7\cdot10^{-14}$ (\fichier{outils/genome/eclaireur/synonyme.py}, \texttt{facteur\_inverse}) \mes.

\paragraph{The entropy constraint.} The symbol $k$ of column $j$ minimises
\begin{equation}\label{t3ecl:eq:lagrange}
\bigl(|w_j| - c_k\, s_{b(j)}\bigr)^2 + \lambda\, \bar c\, U_{jj}^2\, \ell(k), \qquad \ell(k) = -\log_2 p(k),
\end{equation}
$p$ being the law of the symbols of the frontier base of the tensor, $\bar c$ the mean error per weight of the frontier in the units of $H$. Divided by $U_{jj}^2$, this is, by~\eqref{t3ecl:eq:obs}, ``increase of the output error $+\,\lambda\,\bar c$ bits'': $\lambda$ is the price of a bit in units of frontier error. With $\lambda = 0$, it is feedback alone.

\paragraph{The decoder does not change.} Same eight symbols, same four centres, same scales: the mechanism lives entirely in the coder, and its cost of transcription is zero.

\begin{proposition}[The control that can fail: $H = I$ reproduces the frontier]\label{t3ecl:prop:temoin}
If $H = I$ and $\lambda = 0$, the synonymous mutation chooses, for each weight, the symbol of the frontier base.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
With $H = I$, $H^{-1} = I$ and $U = I$: no correction is carried over, and each weight chooses the centre nearest to $|w_j|/s_{b(j)}$, with the sign of $w_j$. The centres are the centroids of a \emph{contiguous} partition of the magnitudes, $\{0,\,0.5\}$, $\{1,\,1.5,\,2\}$, $\{3,\,4\}$, $\{6\}$, with centres $0.237$; $1.424$; $3.419$; $6$. The Voronoi boundaries, midpoints of two neighbouring centres, are $0.831$; $2.421$; $4.709$ \calc. Each magnitude falls into the cell of its class: $0.5 < 0.831 < 1$, $2 < 2.421 < 3$, $4 < 4.709 < 6$. The symbol chosen is therefore that of the frontier function.
\end{proof}

Measured (control T1): 0 symbols different out of 8\,294\,400; with the real $H$, 1\,789\,132 \mes.

\subsection{The protocol}

Experts: layers 0, 8, 16 and 23, experts 0, 4, \ldots, 28 (96 matrices, 796 million weights); later passes on subsets declared before them. Calibration: windows 10 to 21; held-out evaluation: windows 0 to 9; output errors weighted by $g^2$. The \emph{expert} output error replaces the three matrices at once, with the bounded SwiGLU of gpt-oss between them. \textbf{Criterion, declared beforehand} (head of \texttt{synonyme.py}): rate at most that of the frontier, with the same estimator, \emph{and} expert output error lower on each of the two halves of the evaluation and in each of the four layers.

\paragraph{What this says about the structure.} The error on the weights \emph{rises} (from 4.3 to 4.9\,\%) while the output error is divided by three to four. This is the conclusion of \cref{t3que:sec:prediction}, measured.

\paragraph{The price, in one number.} The exact complement behind the base costs 1.689 bits instead of 1.219: the base is no longer a function of the code ($H(\text{base}\mid\text{code}) > 0$). Its use is that of a \textbf{second genome}: an exact file (the canonical code, 3.706 bits) and a separate synonym base (2.60 bits read), the disk paying for the room of both (about 6.3 bits per weight stored), the engine reading only one of them per expert miss. This is the ``fast mode and exact mode'' of Stone's idea; \cref{t3sno:ch} builds it.

It weighs heavily in layer 8 and is the first candidate to remain exact.

\section{The end-to-end judgement: the KL against the f64 reference}\label{t3ecl:sec:kl}

\paragraph{The judge, in five sentences.} At each position $t$ of a text, a language model returns a probability law over the next token; let $p_t$ be that of the f64 reference of lot R of Volume~II (the exact model, computed in double precision) and $q_t$ that of the model in which experts have been replaced. The \textbf{KL of the position} is the Kullback--Leibler divergence~\cite{kullback1951}
\begin{equation}\label{t3ecl:eq:kl}
\mathrm{KL}_t = \sum_i p_{t,i} \log \frac{p_{t,i}}{q_{t,i}} \quad \text{(in nats)},
\end{equation}
zero when the two laws are equal, and otherwise equal, up to the cut term, to the mean extra cost, in nats per token, of predicting with $q_t$ a text drawn from $p_t$. The instrument \texttt{juge kl} sums only the tokens $i$ such that $\log p_{t,i} > -16$: those to which the reference gives less than $e^{-16} \approx 1.1\cdot10^{-7}$ \calc{} are left aside, everywhere and for all models; without renormalisation, the sum so cut can be very slightly negative. The \textbf{KL of a pass} is the mean of $\mathrm{KL}_t$ over the scored positions; here, over the ten evaluation windows of wiki.test that Volume~II calls R1, that is 2\,550 positions (255 per window). It is the judge of the whole volume: an error on the weights or at the output of an expert is only a clue to it, and \cref{t3syn:def:kl} takes it up again with its two parts, the smooth part and the tail of routing flips (\cref{t3syn:sec:juge}).

\paragraph{The protocol (P1 of the round).} The \texttt{kl} subcommand of the fork, then \texttt{juge kl} of lot R of Volume~II: the 10 windows of R1 (2\,550 scored positions), the f64 reference, experts 0, 4, \ldots, 28 of layers 0, 8, 16 and 23 replaced by their base, that is \textbf{32 experts out of 768}, each served at each position that routes it, against \texttt{ref-openai-20b.kld}. The divergence~\cite{kullback1951} is read position by position, in nats; the identical first choice is given with its Wilson interval~\cite{wilson1927},
\begin{equation}\label{t3ecl:eq:wilson}
\frac{\hat p + \frac{z^2}{2n} \pm z\sqrt{\frac{\hat p (1-\hat p)}{n} + \frac{z^2}{4n^2}}}{1 + \frac{z^2}{n}}, \qquad z = 1.96 ;
\end{equation}
for $\hat p = 0.8714$ and $n = 2\,550$, it gives $[0.8578,\ 0.8838]$ \calc, the interval that the log carries. Threshold, declared beforehand: the synonymous mutation is better end to end if its mean KL is below that of the frontier \emph{and} its rate of identical first choice is above.

\paragraph{Which variant was judged.} The pass was written before the result of $\lambda = 1$: it judges the variant $\lambda = 0$, $\delta^\ast = 1$, at rate $+0.011$ bit, whose output errors are of the same order. The KL was measured on the variant $\lambda = 0$, at 2.649 bits; $\lambda = 1$ (2.563 bits) was judged only by the expert output error. The synthesis of the round had to correct an attribution of this KL to $\lambda = 1$ (\cref{t3syn:sec:frontiere}). \textbf{No variant of the synonymous mutation is therefore measured both below the frontier in bits and below it in KL.}

\paragraph{What the KL says about the frontier itself.} Replacing 32 experts out of 768 (4\,\% of the experts, in 4 layers out of 24) by the 2.64-bit base already costs a KL of 0.036, six times the threshold of architecture B (0.0055, fondations/07 §\,4.2); the synonymous mutation brings it down to 0.026, still four times above. The base, even a synonym one, is a \textbf{fast mode} or a \textbf{draft}, not a silent replacement for the exact mode.

Seen from the weights, gpt-oss is noise: codes independent to within 0.005 bit, sign of the zeros at 1.0000 bit of entropy, and a rate--distortion bound that only a better quantiser or a vector code approaches, with nothing to ``generate''. The two mechanisms that win (\cref{t3ecl:tab:verdict}) add no structure to the weights: the first \textbf{removes information that the computation never reads}, the second \textbf{moves the error to where the real inputs do not see it}. \begin{faux}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The zero codon and the fingerprint of the experts.} The \texttt{.trc} format checks the fingerprint of each decoded expert against the bytes of the GGUF; a canonical expert has other bytes. A fingerprint of the canonical code is needed: a decision of format, which \cref{t3cod:ch} takes.
\item \textbf{Bit exactness} is proved for an IEEE dot product in fixed order and measured on the f64 reference (24 tokens at the logit, 2\,550 positions at the fingerprint of the \texttt{.kld}); for the kernels of the engine, it is proposition A1 of \cref{t3cod:ch} and its measurement.
\item \textbf{The subset.} 4 layers out of 24, 8 then 4 experts out of 32 per layer; the gain of the synonymous mutation varies a great deal from one layer to another ($\times 0.97$ to $\times 0.23$); a layer where it would reverse is not excluded. The KL substitutes only 32 experts out of 768.
\item \textbf{The rate estimator} of the synonymous mutation is the entropy ($H_0$ or $H\mid\kappa$), not a real coder. For the frontier, the real coder gives 2.6441 against $2.6066$ + scales; nothing indicates that the gap is different for the synonym symbols, but it is not measured.
\item \textbf{$\delta^\ast$} was chosen on expert 2 of the four layers: other experts than those measured, but of the same layers.
\item \textbf{The calibration and the evaluation} come from the same corpus (wiki.test), disjoint windows; an evaluation on another domain (code, dialogue) could reduce the gain.
\item \textbf{The two comparisons} are made on different subsets ($\lambda = 1$ on 4 experts per layer, $\lambda = 0$ on 8), each against its own frontier.
\item \textbf{The KL judges $\lambda = 0$, not $\lambda = 1$}: the 0.078-bit gain of $\lambda = 1$ is judged only by the expert output error, and the round showed that this error does not predict the KL (\cref{t3syn:ch}).
\item \textbf{The bound of \cref{t3que:sec:frontiere}} assumes the codes independent given the scale; an unmeasured dependency would lower it.
\end{itemize}
\end{faux}
