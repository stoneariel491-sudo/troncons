\begin{enbref}
Two moves of the nervous system served as guides, as analogies. The retina does not transmit the image, it transmits what distinguishes it from what its neighbours predicted: that is predictive coding~\cite{rao1999}, and a neuron of the fly's eye even adjusts its response to the law of its inputs to draw the most information from them~\cite{genome-pe-laughlin1981}. The developing brain, for its part, makes too many synapses and then removes those that activity does not call upon: that is synaptic pruning~\cite{huttenlocher1979}. Transposed to the weights, the first move asks what the neighbours of a code say about it, and the answer is: almost nothing, except the column through which it reads its input. The second move is formulated exactly in rate theory: set a price for the bit, and let the synapses that do not pay it disappear. It gains a great deal on weight metrics, and almost all of that gain vanishes when judged on text. What holds is modest and solid: a zero sign that one stops paying for, a map of columns, and a base that saves 9\,\% of the bits without degrading the divergence beyond the noise of the measurement.
\end{enbref}

\section{The synonymous codons of E2M1}\label{t3pre:sec:codons}

\paragraph{The fact} \mes. On the 12 tensors, $P(+0) = 0.068\,46$ and $P(-0) = 0.068\,48$; more broadly, $P(+c) = P(-c)$ to within $10^{-4}$ for each magnitude. Per tensor, $P(+0)$ and $P(-0)$ differ by less than $10^{-4}$, from 0.059 to 0.098 each (\fichier{histo-12-gptoss20b.npz}). The quantiser that produced the MXFP4 of gpt-oss keeps the sign of the original value even when its magnitude rounds to zero. Yet E2M1 has two codes for zero, \texttt{0b0000} and \texttt{0b1000}; ggml reads both as the integer 0, the f64 reference as $+0$ and $-0$, which change no non-zero sum (\cref{t3ecl:prop:zero}).

\paragraph{The biological parallel, as an image.} The synonymous codons of the genetic code (\cref{t3int:sec:genome}) often differ by their third base, Crick's ``wobble'' position~\cite{genome-pe-crick1966wobble}, and a mutation that goes from one to the other is silent. The two codes $\pm0$ are two synonymous codons; the information that distinguishes them has no phenotype. Canonicalising every $-0$ into $+0$ before entropy coding amounts to reducing codon usage to a single codon per meaning.

\begin{proposition}[The exact gain of folding]\label{t3pre:prop:fondu}
Let $C$ be the 4-bit code of a weight and $V = v(C)$ its value, $v(c) = (-1)^{c_3} K[c \mathbin{\&} 7]$, injective except on $\{0, 8\}$. Let $S$ be the sign bit. Then
\begin{equation}\label{t3pre:eq:fondu}
H(C) = H(V) + P(V = 0)\, H(S \mid V = 0).
\end{equation}
The same statement holds conditionally on any context the decoder knows (scale class $\kappa$, neighbouring byte).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$V$ is a function of $C$, so $H(V \mid C) = 0$ and $H(C, V) = H(C)$. By the chain rule, $H(C, V) = H(V) + H(C \mid V)$. Given $V = v \ne 0$, $C$ is determined ($v$ injective outside 0): $H(C \mid V = v) = 0$. Given $V = 0$, $C \in \{0, 8\}$ is determined by $S$: $H(C\mid V = 0) = H(S \mid V = 0)$. Hence $H(C\mid V) = \sum_v P(V = v)\,H(C\mid V = v) = P(V = 0)\,H(S\mid V = 0)$. Replacing $H(\cdot)$ by $H(\cdot\mid\kappa)$ everywhere changes no step.
\end{proof}

Folding 8 into 0 replaces $C$ by $C'$ of the same value, and $H(C') = H(V)$: the gain is $P(V = 0)\,H(S\mid V = 0)$, that is roughly $P(V = 0)$ bit when the sign is a fair coin. \textbf{Computation set out} \calc: $P(V = 0) = 0.068\,46 + 0.068\,48 = 0.136\,94$; $H(S\mid V = 0) = h(0.068\,46/0.136\,94) = h(0.499\,93) = 1.0000$ bit, $h$ being the binary entropy; gain $= 0.136\,94$ bit per weight. Measured by the conditional entropy (context $\kappa$, tables per tensor): 0.136\,939 \mes; by the real codec: 0.1370 \mes. The form of the gain per byte, which serves the format, is taken up in \cref{t3cod:ch}.

\section{Lossless predictive coding: what the neighbours say about a code}\label{t3pre:sec:predictif}

\paragraph{The frame.} For a discrete alphabet, ``predict, then code the deviation'' is written exactly: code the code with a law conditioned on a context $X$ that the decoder knows. The gain is the mutual information
\begin{equation}\label{t3pre:eq:im}
I(C ; X \mid \kappa) = H(C \mid \kappa) - H(C \mid \kappa, X).
\end{equation}
The frequency estimator is biased downwards; by Miller's expansion~\cite{miller1955bias}, with tables per tensor, the bias on the gain is $(K_X - K_\kappa)(16 - 1)/(2 N_t \ln 2)$ bit per symbol, $K$ being the number of contexts and $N_t = 265\,420\,800$ weights per tensor. \textbf{Threshold declared beforehand}: 0.015 bit per weight, the header cost of one more stream (0.40\,\% of 3.84 bits, study 03).

\paragraph{Reading} (\cref{t3pre:tab:contextes}).
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The neighbours predict almost nothing.} Given the scale of its block, an MXFP4 code is an almost independent draw; what the literature calls the ``spatial dependency'' of the weights is here below 0.01 bit.
\item \textbf{The parent expert predicts nothing} (0.004): the experts of a layer are not mutated copies of one another at the level of the codes, which \cref{t3dup:ch} puts in numbers on the values, with permutation.
\item \textbf{Each block contains at least one large code} ($|\mathrm{E2M1}| \ge 2$): 0 blocks out of 99\,532\,800 lack one; the OCP rule of the scale ($2^{\lfloor\log_2 \max\rfloor - 2}$) is indeed followed, but the constraint is worth only $10^{-4}$ bit: a block almost always has several large codes.
\item \textbf{The input column carries a structure.} Some columns have, on average over all the rows and all the experts of a tensor, larger weights relative to the scale of their block. The ratio between the most energetic column and the median ranges from 1.11 (blk.0 down) to 2.93 (blk.23 gate), in root energy. The inputs of gate and up are the hidden state, some of whose channels carry outlier activations; training answered with weights of different size. It is a fact of the input, shared by the 32 experts: a map of 2 bits per column and per tensor (720 bytes) suffices. It is the same statistic that the chance parent of \cref{t3dup:ch} took back at 88 to 94\,\% in layer 0.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{The controls, and a struck-out correction.} Context equal to the code itself: $H = 0$ (holds). ``Left'' context randomly permuted: gain of $6.33\cdot10^{-5}$ for an expected bias of $6.26\cdot10^{-5}$ (holds, \fichier{contextes-temoin-corrige.json}). The deliverable carries a first formula, struck out: ``\barre{Expected bias $(51 - 1)\cdot15/(2N\ln 2) = 5.4\cdot10^{-6}$ with pooled $N$, control failed.}'' Corrected on 2026-10-16: the tables are per tensor, the bias is computed with $N_t$ and $51 - 3$ added parameters; with the old formula (the break of the corrected control), the control does fail (\fichier{contextes-temoin-corrige-casse.json}).

\paragraph{The real codec, $\kappa \times$ column.} Streams of nibbles separated by context, alphabet of 16, zero folded, map and tables counted (\fichier{taux-reel-colonne-12-gptoss20b.json}) \mes: \textbf{3.6844 bits per weight}, against 3.7103 for the same split in $\kappa$ alone (real gain of the column: 0.0259) and 3.7064 for the byte alphabet in $\kappa$. Per tensor, the column saves nothing on blk.23.ffn\_down (3.6744 $\to$ 3.6741: the input of down is the output of a SwiGLU, with no outlier channel) and most of it on gate and up. The system lossless in value, zero folded and column, therefore reads \textbf{3.6844 bits against 3.8434}, that is $-4.1\,\%$ \calc, and \cref{t3syn:ch} shows where this gain lives (almost all of it in layer 0).

\section{Pruning and bounded predictive quantisation: the rate--distortion frame}\label{t3pre:sec:cadre}

\paragraph{The problem.} Replace each code $c$, in a block of scale $e$, by a cell $s$ of a partition $P_k$ of the 16 codes ordered on the real line ($-12, \ldots, -1, -0, +0, +1, \ldots, +12$ in doubled E2M1 magnitudes $K$), chosen according to a context $k$ that the decoder knows, rebuilt as $\hat w = r_{k,s}\, 2^{e-128}$. Rate: $H(s \mid k)$, one table per tensor and per context. Distortion: the sum of the $(w - \hat w)^2$. It is an entropy-constrained quantiser on a discrete source~\cite{genome-pe-chou1989ecvq,genome-pe-farvardin1984}.

\begin{lemme}[The optimal centre of a cell]\label{t3pre:lem:centre}
For a fixed cell, the weighted centroid $r^\ast = B/A$ is the reconstruction that minimises $\sum_i 4^{\,e_i - 128}(K_i - r)^2$, and the remaining error is $C - B^2/A$, with $A = \sum_i 4^{e_i}$, $B = \sum_i 4^{e_i} K_i$, $C = \sum_i 4^{e_i} K_i^2$ (up to a factor $4^{-128}$).
\end{lemme}

\begin{proof}
The function $r \mapsto C - 2rB + r^2 A$ is a quadratic in $r$, with derivative $2(Ar - B)$, zero at $B/A$; $A > 0$ makes it a minimum, where the value is $C - 2B^2/A + B^2/A = C - B^2/A$. (The vector case is that of Chou, Lookabaugh and Gray~\cite{genome-pe-chou1989ecvq}.)
\end{proof}

\begin{lemme}[Exact dynamic programming]\label{t3pre:lem:dp}
With a multiplier $\mu$, the cost of a partition into intervals of the 16 ordered positions is a sum of terms each depending only on its cell, $c(i, j) = D_{[i,j]}/E + \mu\, n_{[i,j]} \bigl(-\log_2 (n_{[i,j]}/n_k)\bigr)$, $E$ being the energy of the context and $n$ the counts. The best partition is obtained exactly by the recurrence
\begin{equation}\label{t3pre:eq:dp}
J(0) = 0, \qquad J(j) = \min_{0 \le i < j}\ J(i) + c(i, j - 1),
\end{equation}
in $16 \times 17 / 2 = 136$ cell evaluations, instead of the $2^{15}$ partitions.
\end{lemme}

\begin{proof}
A partition into intervals of $\{0, \ldots, j-1\}$ ends with a last interval $[i, j-1]$, preceded by a partition of $\{0, \ldots, i-1\}$. The cost being additive by cell, the minimal cost decomposes as~\eqref{t3pre:eq:dp} (Bellman's principle). There is one possible cell per pair $i \le j$ of the 16 positions, that is 136, each evaluated once by \cref{t3pre:lem:centre} on cumulative sums. This is the histogram segmentation of Muresan and Effros~\cite{genome-pe-muresan2002}, and of Wu and Zhang~\cite{genome-pe-wu1993}. The cells are intervals because, for the squared distortion, an optimal cell is convex on the line.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Reverse water-filling, and pruning as its zero-rate regime]\label{t3pre:prop:remplissage}
A $\mu$ common to all the contexts achieves the minimum of the total distortion under a total rate constraint: at the points where each context is on its convex curve $D_k(R_k)$, the Karush--Kuhn--Tucker condition is $-\partial D_k/\partial R_k = \mu$ for every active context, and a context whose slope at zero rate is weaker than $\mu$ stays at zero rate. The low-energy contexts (small scale $e$, little-active column) reach their zero rate first: a single cell, the whole context rebuilt by its centre, almost zero.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
We minimise $\sum_k D_k(R_k)$ under $\sum_k R_k \le R$, $R_k \ge 0$, with convex decreasing $D_k$. The Lagrangian $\sum_k D_k(R_k) + \mu(\sum_k R_k - R) - \sum_k \nu_k R_k$ gives, at the optimum, $D_k'(R_k) + \mu - \nu_k = 0$, $\nu_k \ge 0$, $\nu_k R_k = 0$. For an active context ($R_k > 0$), $\nu_k = 0$ and $-D'_k(R_k) = \mu$; for an inactive context, $-D'_k(0) = \mu - \nu_k \le \mu$. Convexity makes these conditions sufficient. This is the reverse water-filling of~\cite[th. 10.3.3]{cover2006}, transposed to a discrete source by contexts. A low-energy context has a slope $-D'_k(0)$ proportional to its energy: it becomes inactive first.
\end{proof}

\textbf{Synaptic pruning thus appears as the zero-rate regime of reverse water-filling}: one does not choose to prune, one chooses a price for the bit, and the synapses that do not pay it disappear. This is the move of Optimal Brain Damage~\cite{lecun1989obd} and of Deep Compression~\cite{han2015deepcompression}, written on our cells.

\paragraph{The metric, and why that of the frontier is wrong here.} The first pass used the global error of the frontier, $\sum (w - \hat w)^2 / \sum w^2$ over the 12 tensors. Yet the share of the energy per tensor is extremely unequal \mes: blk.23.ffn\_down 91.5\,\%, blk.16.ffn\_down 8.0\,\%, blk.8.ffn\_down 0.40\,\%, the other nine together 0.015\,\%. A common $\mu$ under this metric empties the nine small tensors: 0.32 bit ``at 4.29\,\%'' (\fichier{ecsq\_e\_metrique\_globale}, for the record), absurd. \textbf{Metric retained}: the mean over the tensors of their own relative error, each matrix counting as much; the base is 4.3367\,\% in it (from 4.05 to 4.80\,\% per tensor).

\paragraph{Why pruning by magnitude loses} (\cref{t3pre:tab:rd}). Pruning means folding the small magnitudes into the zero symbol; the rate falls only by the entropy of these symbols, while all the other codes keep their 3 bits and more. At equal error, it is better to enlarge \emph{all} the cells a little than to empty a few. The small magnitudes carry little energy, but also few bits: the 50\,\% smallest weights of a tensor carry only 4.3\,\% of its energy, and removing them removes only about half the bits that would be needed. Reverse water-filling by $e$, for its part, gains 23\,\% of the bits on the weight metric; \cref{t3pre:sec:sortie} says what is left of it.

\section{Activity-dependent pruning}\label{t3pre:sec:activite}

\paragraph{The frame.} For a matrix $W$ and inputs $x$ of covariance $\Sigma$, by~\eqref{t3que:eq:sortie},
\begin{equation}\label{t3pre:eq:diag}
\E\,\|\Delta W x\|^2 = \mathrm{tr}(\Delta W\, \Sigma\, \Delta W^\top) = \sum_{i,j,j'} \Delta W_{ij}\Delta W_{ij'}\Sigma_{jj'} \ \approx\ \sum_{i,j} \Delta W_{ij}^2\, \Sigma_{jj},
\end{equation}
a diagonal approximation, exact on average if the errors of different columns are uncorrelated. The sensitivity of a weight is $s_j = \E[x_j^2]$, the activity of its column. This is the criterion of Wanda ($|W_{ij}|\cdot\|X_j\|$)~\cite{genome-pe-sun2023wanda}, close to that of SparseGPT~\cite{genome-pe-frantar2023sparsegpt}, and the diagonal term of OBD. The biological analogy is explicit: in the developing brain, synaptic pruning depends on activity, a little-used synapse is removed first.

\paragraph{The system.} The masses of \cref{t3pre:lem:centre} become $A = \sum 4^{e} s_j$; the context becomes $(e, q)$, $q$ being the activity quartile of the column in the expert (a map of 2 bits per column and per expert matrix, 0.0007 bit per weight, counted). The $s_j$ come from windows 0 to 4 of the corpus of lot R, captured by the f64 reference (inputs of gate and up: the normalised state routed to the expert; input of down: the SwiGLU computed with the exact weights).

\paragraph{What the measurement revealed} (\cref{t3pre:tab:sortie}). The plans that fill across the absolute scales keep each matrix below 3\,\% relative error, and the whole expert becomes wrong at 22 to 35\,\%. The cause: the quantity that the relative error of a matrix measures is not the one that counts. A row $i$ of gate or of up acts on the output through column $i$ of down, and training distributes the scale freely between the two: the up path has a rescaling symmetry, $u_i \to c\,u_i$ and $W_d[:, i] \to W_d[:, i]/c$ change almost nothing (almost, because the bound on $u$ in the SwiGLU is not homogeneous, \cref{t3dup:prop:permutation}). A row of small scale in gate can therefore weigh heavily at the output, and reverse water-filling by matrix empties precisely these rows.

\paragraph{Hebbian sensitivity.} It answers at first order (\fichier{histo\_hebb.py}). Linearising the output around the exact weights,
\begin{equation}\label{t3pre:eq:hebb}
\E\,\|\Delta y\|^2 \approx \sum_{i,j} \Delta W_{ij}^2\, r_i\, s_j, \qquad s_j = \E[x_j^2], \quad r_i = \|W_d[:, i]\|^2\, \E\Bigl[\Bigl(\frac{\partial a_i}{\partial g_i}\Bigr)^2\Bigr]
\end{equation}
(or $\partial a_i / \partial u_i$ for the up matrix), $a$ being the intermediate activation. The first factor, $s_j$, is the activity of the input (presynaptic); the second, $r_i$, the influence of the output (postsynaptic); the weight of a synapse is their product, Hebb's product. The factorisation $\E[(\partial a/\partial g)^2 x^2] \approx \E[(\partial a/\partial g)^2]\,\E[x^2]$ neglects their correlation, and the diagonal approximation~\eqref{t3pre:eq:diag} that of the columns: two hypotheses that the measurement judges.

\subsection{Hebbian pruning: what holds, what overfits}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Hebbian masses, context $e$ alone} (no map sent: $r_i$ and $s_j$ serve only the coder, to choose the partitions and the centres): \textbf{2.1815 real bits, expert error 5.70\,\%} against 5.57\,\% for the base at 2.6444, that is 0.825 times the bits for the same error to within 2\,\% \mes. The same reverse water-filling without Hebbian sensitivity broke the expert (22\,\%): it was indeed the gate/up $\leftrightarrow$ down scale symmetry that broke it, and the postsynaptic influence that repairs it.
\item \textbf{With maps of rows and columns} (16 classes, 9\,273 cells): predicted 4.33\,\%, measured \textbf{9.23\,\%}, layer 8 at 17.8\,\%. The prediction does not hold: the quartiles of $r_i$ and $s_j$ are estimated on about 320 inputs per expert (windows 0 to 4), and the factorisation of~\eqref{t3pre:eq:hebb} neglects their correlation; a plan that gives zero bits to the classes judged inactive pays dearly for any estimation error. Overfitting measured, not supposed.
\item \textbf{Variant relative to the row} ($\delta \times$ row $\times$ column): 1.9619 real bits, KL 0.091, paired difference $+0.027 \pm 0.004$ against the base (\fichier{kl/comparer-base-hebb\_d\_lc-f5-9.json}): loses.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{What the KL decides} (\cref{t3pre:tab:kl,t3pre:tab:apparie}).
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Every plan that fills across the absolute scales loses}, by 3 to 5 times the KL of the base: partitions by $e$ (0.309), pruning by column activity (0.271), Hebbian by $e$ (0.195). The plans that break the expert break the model.
\item \textbf{Even at equal expert error.} Hebbian by $e$ makes 5.70\,\% expert error against 5.57\,\% for the base, and three times its KL (paired $+0.131 \pm 0.007$, first choice $-20.8$ points). The mean relative error at the output of an expert, weighted by $\|y\|^2$, is not enough to judge: it is held by the tokens and the dimensions of large output; reverse water-filling across the scales concentrates the error on what is small (blocks and rows of small scale, 23\,\% of the weights folded into zero), which the RMS norm of the following layers brings back to scale and which the KL sees. The lesson of the subject: at the output, one should judge the relative error \emph{per token}, or the KL directly, not the total energy.
\item \textbf{At equal mean error, the $\kappa$ plan is not worth the base in KL} ($+0.0167 \pm 0.0049$, 3.4\,$\sigma$): it moves the error towards layer 0 (6.1\,\% expert error against 5.1\,\%), and an early error in the network weighs more in the KL. Hence the plan ``each tensor at the error of the base'' (\fichier{plans.py --par-tenseur}, one $\mu$ per tensor: domination tensor by tensor), at \textbf{2.4079 real bits, 0.911 times the base}. Paired against the base: $+0.0066 \pm 0.0053$ (1.2\,$\sigma$), first choice $-2.1 \pm 1.1$ points. \textbf{Statistically equal to the base} by the rule of lot R; literally a little above.
\end{itemize}

This is the lossy point that the subject retains: 9\,\% fewer bits than the base of the frontier, at a KL equal within the noise of the measurement. The cautious reading is that of the deliverable itself: the central paired difference remains positive, and with more positions it could become significant. \Cref{t3syn:ch} places this point on the common frontier of the round, under the name ``partitions by $\kappa$, equal by the rule''.

\section{What remains}\label{t3pre:sec:reste}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The card kernels}: a decoder with a table per $(\kappa,$ column$)$ or per $(e, q)$, and the reconstruction by a table of centres (4 to 16 registers); their connection into the coder of the format. The lossless part (zero folded and column) has entered the format v1.2 (\cref{t3cod:ch}).
\item \textbf{Error feedback} (GPTQ, ``closed-loop predictive coding'') on the alphabet of the cells, with the full Hessian of the real inputs; and a sensitivity criterion judged per token, or learnt against the KL, since the mean energy misled.
\item \textbf{The dominated $\kappa$ plan} + column + zero folded on the 24 layers, and its KL; then gpt-oss-120b.
\end{itemize}

\begin{faux}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The lossy rates are ideal entropies}, not a codec, except where the ``real bits'' column says so; the gap of the real codec measured on the base is 0.46\,\%, and it may be larger for tables with many contexts (2\,077 cells in $e$, 3\,808 in $(e, q)$) and rare cells.
\item \textbf{The diagonal approximation} of the sensitivity~\eqref{t3pre:eq:diag} neglects the correlations between input columns, and the Hebbian factorisation~\eqref{t3pre:eq:hebb} those between input and derivative; the exact measurement at the output judges them, and made a prediction fail (struck out).
\item \textbf{The inputs}: 10 windows of 512 tokens from a single corpus; about 640 inputs per expert and per layer, about 320 for calibration: the activity quartiles of a little-routed expert are noisy.
\item \textbf{The KL alters only 4 layers out of 24}; the effect on the 24 is not measured (the KL would grow, for the base as for the plan). It covers 1\,275 positions of a single corpus, and the 9\,\% advantage of the dominated $\kappa$ plan is at a statistically \emph{equal} KL, not a better one (central difference $+0.0066$).
\item \textbf{The gain of the synonymous codons} assumes that every engine reads $\pm0$ as 0: true of ggml (integer table) and of the reference; for the kernels of troncons, it is proposition A1 of \cref{t3cod:ch} and its measurement.

\end{itemize}
