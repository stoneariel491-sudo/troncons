\chapter{Sparsity and speculative decoding}\label{ch:sparsite}

\begin{origine}
\textbf{At the start}, two families of techniques promised to read fewer bytes per token: activation sparsity (read only the useful columns) and speculative decoding (read the weights once for several tokens), both published on machines where RAM holds the model.
\textbf{This chapter establishes} the amortization theorem, $\mathcal E(\alpha,k) = (1-\alpha^{k+1})/(1-\alpha)$ tokens per read of the weights, and its corollaries; the exact condition under which speculation pays for a mixture of experts read from disk, which independent routing never satisfies; the measurement, on a SwiGLU model, that sparsity and speculation do not compose; and the acceptance rates measured on the Qwen3.5 family.
\textbf{What remains} is the real expert-union curve of a 2026 MoE on draft tokens, the agreement of the native MTP module, and tree verification under Vulkan.
Source: \fichier{fondations/05-sparsite.md}, \fichier{fondations/journaux/05-sparsite/}.
\end{origine}

\section{The judge, and the lever with the right slope}\label{sec:spec-juge}

A pass of the target over $n$ tokens (a verification batch) costs, with perfectly overlapped stages,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:passe}
T(n) = \max\Bigl( \frac{O_{\mathrm{disk}}(n)}{W_{\mathrm{disk}}},\ \frac{O_{\mathrm{RAM}}(n)}{W_{\mathrm{PCIe}}},\ \frac{O_{\mathrm{VRAM}}(n)}{W_{\mathrm{VRAM}}},\ \frac{C(n)}{F} \Bigr),
\end{equation}
and, without overlap, the sum of the same terms (\cref{thm:toit}). $O_x(n)$ is the number of weight bytes the pass reads from stage $x$, $C(n) \approx 2 n N_{\text{active}}$ the number of operations, $F$ the effective compute throughput. For a \textbf{dense} model, $O_x(n)$ does not depend on $n$: that is the whole point. For an \textbf{MoE} or a model with \textbf{sparse activations}, $O_x(n)$ grows with $n$, because the pass reads the union of what its $n$ tokens request.

Token throughput is $\mathcal E / (T(n) + T_{\text{draft}})$, where $\mathcal E$ is the number of tokens accepted per pass. Three levers: reduce $O(n)$ (sparsity, hot neurons, expert prediction); increase $\mathcal E$ at almost unchanged $T(n)$ (speculative decoding); hide $T_{\text{draft}}$ inside $T(n)$. When weights come from disk, $T(n)$ is counted in seconds and $T_{\text{draft}}$ in milliseconds: \textbf{the second lever crushes the other two, on one condition, that $O(n)$ stays flat in $n$}. Everything that follows quantifies that condition.

\begin{proposition}[The compute knee]\label{prop:coude}
For a dense model of $N$ active weights at $b$ bytes per weight, read from a stage of throughput $W$, verifying $n$ tokens stays bounded by reading as long as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coude}
n \;<\; n^\star = \frac{F\, b}{2\, W}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Reading costs $N b / W$ seconds whatever $n$; computing costs $2 n N / F$. The latter stays below the former as long as $2nN/F < Nb/W$, i.e.\ $n < Fb/(2W)$.
\end{proof}

With $F \approx 20$\,Tflop/s (order of magnitude of the FP16 tensor cores of an RTX 4060 Laptop, not measured, and lowered by dequantization) and $b = 0.6$ byte per weight (about 4.8 bits) \calc: $n^\star \approx 24$ if weights are in VRAM (251.3\,GB/s), $\approx 490$ if they cross the link (12.2\,GB/s; the study, with the link loaded at 6.38\,GB/s, gave 940), $\approx 2\,100$ if they come from disk (2.82\,GB/s). This table says why SpecExec verifies trees of 1\,024 to 2\,048 tokens in the offloaded regime, and why EAGLE on a card that holds the model stops at a few dozen: it is not a choice, it is $n^\star$. The measurement of \cref{fig:cn} confirms it despite its noise: up to 32 tokens, an all-in-VRAM pass costs at most 1.35 times a one-token pass.

\section{Speculative decoding}\label{sec:speculatif}

\subsection{The verification rule}

Leviathan, Kalman and Matias~\cite{leviathan2023} and Chen et al.~\cite{chen2023spec} state the same rule. A draft with law $q$ proposes $x_1, \dots, x_k$; the target $p$ computes in one pass $p(\cdot \mid \text{prefix}, x_1, \dots, x_i)$ for $i = 0, \dots, k$. Each $x_i$ is accepted with probability $\min(1, p(x_i)/q(x_i))$; at the first refusal, a token is drawn from the residual law $r = (p - q)^+ / \lVert (p - q)^+ \rVert_1$; if everything is accepted, one more token is drawn from $p$.

\begin{proposition}[Speculation is exact]\label{prop:exacte}
The token emitted at each position follows exactly the target law $p$, and the acceptance probability of a position is $\alpha = \sum_x \min(p(x), q(x)) = 1 - \tfrac12 \lVert p - q \rVert_1$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The probability of emitting $x$ is that of proposing and accepting it, plus that of refusing then drawing $x$ from $r$:
\[
q(x) \min\Bigl(1, \frac{p(x)}{q(x)}\Bigr) + (1-\alpha)\, \frac{(p(x) - q(x))^+}{1 - \alpha} = \min(p(x), q(x)) + (p(x) - q(x))^+ = p(x),
\]
using $\lVert (p-q)^+ \rVert_1 = \sum_x (p(x) - \min(p(x),q(x))) = 1 - \alpha$. The acceptance probability is $\sum_x q(x)\min(1, p(x)/q(x)) = \sum_x \min(p(x), q(x))$, and $\sum_x \min(p,q) = 1 - \tfrac12 \sum_x |p - q|$ since $\min(a,b) = \tfrac12(a + b - |a-b|)$.
\end{proof}

In greedy decoding, a token is accepted if it is the target's argmax; $\alpha$ is then the argmax agreement rate, measured in \cref{sec:alpha}.

\subsection{The amortization theorem}

\begin{theoreme}[Tokens per read of the weights]\label{thm:amortissement}
If each of the $k$ drafted positions is accepted with the same probability $\alpha < 1$, independently of the others and as long as none has been refused, the number $N$ of tokens produced by one verification pass (one full read of the active weights) has expectation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:amortissement}
\mathcal E(\alpha, k) \;=\; \E[N] \;=\; \sum_{i=0}^{k} \alpha^i \;=\; \frac{1 - \alpha^{k+1}}{1 - \alpha}.
\end{equation}
\end{theoreme}

\begin{proof}
Let $A$ be the number of drafted tokens accepted before the first refusal, $0 \le A \le k$. The pass always returns one token more than $A$: the correction drawn from $r$ at the first refusal, or the bonus token drawn from $p$ if everything is accepted. Hence $N = A + 1$. The first $i$ positions are all accepted with probability $\alpha^i$, so $\Prob(A \ge i) = \alpha^i$ for $0 \le i \le k$, and $\Prob(A \ge i) = 0$ beyond. For a non-negative integer variable, $\E[A] = \sum_{i \ge 1} \Prob(A \ge i)$; thus $\E[N] = 1 + \sum_{i=1}^{k} \alpha^i = \sum_{i=0}^{k} \alpha^i$, a geometric sum equal to $(1 - \alpha^{k+1})/(1-\alpha)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollaire}[The chain bound]\label{cor:borne-chaine}
$\mathcal E(\alpha, k) < 1/(1 - \alpha)$ for every $k$, and the chain reaches the fraction $1 - \alpha^{k+1}$ of that bound. At $\alpha = 0.8$, $k = 8$ already gives 87\,\% of the bound: beyond, only a better draft or a \emph{tree} raises $\mathcal E$.
\end{corollaire}

\begin{proof}
$\mathcal E(\alpha,k) (1-\alpha) = 1 - \alpha^{k+1} < 1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollaire}[The optimal $k$ when the pass is expensive]\label{cor:k-optimal}
With a pass costing $T$ and a draft costing $t_b$ per proposed token, the throughput $\mathcal E(\alpha,k)/(T + k t_b)$ is maximal at a $k^\star$ that grows as $\log(T/t_b)/\log(1/\alpha)$; for $T = 8$\,s and $t_b = 5$\,ms, $k^\star = 25$ at $\alpha = 0.8$ and $48$ at $\alpha = 0.9$ \calc.
\end{corollaire}

\begin{proof}
The marginal gain of the $(k{+}1)$-th drafted token is $\alpha^{k+1}$ token, its marginal cost $t_b$ for a total cost $T + kt_b$; the optimum roughly satisfies $\alpha^{k^\star} \mathcal E^{-1} \approx t_b/(T + k^\star t_b)$, hence $k^\star \approx \log(T/t_b)/\log(1/\alpha)$ up to logarithmic terms. The numerical values come from the exact maximization of $(1-\alpha^{k+1})/((1-\alpha)(T + kt_b))$ over the integers.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{The control.} The \texttt{amortissement.py} tool simulates 20\,000 chains per case and compares against \cref{eq:amortissement}: gap below 4 standard errors in all 9 cases. With a wrong formula (without the bonus token), 7 cases out of 9 fail \mes. \Cref{fig:amortissement} and \cref{tab:amortissement} give the values.

\subsection{Trees}

With a tree of $K$ nodes, the pass verifies all branches at once (tree attention mask) and keeps the longest accepted path (\cref{fig:speculation}). SpecInfer~\cite{specinfer2023} samples the branches, Sequoia~\cite{sequoia2024} builds the optimal tree by dynamic programming under a budget, SpecExec~\cite{specexec2024} keeps the $K$ most probable continuations of the draft: it is the variant made for the offloaded regime. Its decisive property: $\mathcal E$ is no longer bounded by $1/(1-\alpha)$ but by the coverage of the tree. SpecExec reports up to 20.6 tokens accepted per pass (budget 2\,048) on offloaded Llama 2 70B, and, \textbf{on a desktop RTX 4060, 70B in GPTQ: 9.7 tokens per pass, 3.28 tokens/s, $\times 4.6$} \litt.

\paragraph{The trap of hybrid models.} Qwen3.5, 3.6, 3.8 and Qwen3-Next mix full-attention layers with delta-rule linear-attention layers (Gated DeltaNet), whose state is a matrix per head updated at every token. A refused token cannot be removed from a recurrent state: the previous state must have been kept. For Qwen3.5-9B (32 value heads, $128 \times 128$ state per head, 24 linear layers out of 32) \calc:
\[
\text{state per layer} = 32 \times 128 \times 128 \times 4\ \text{bytes} = 2\,\text{MiB}, \qquad \text{model state} = 24 \times 2 = 48\,\text{MiB per kept position}.
\]
A tree of 256 nodes requires 12\,GiB: impossible on 8\,GB. STree~\cite{stree2025} verifies trees for state-space models by exploiting the structure of their transition matrices; the delta-rule transition, $\alpha_t (I - \beta_t k_t k_t^\top)$, is not diagonal, and no tree verification is published for it.

\section{Mixtures of experts and speculation}\label{sec:moe-spec}

A verification pass of an MoE reads the union of the experts of its $n$ tokens.

\begin{lemme}[The union of experts under independent routing]\label{lem:union}
If each token chooses $k_e$ distinct experts out of $E$, uniformly and independently of the other tokens, the expected number of distinct experts requested by $n$ tokens in a layer is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:union}
U(n) = E\,\Bigl(1 - \bigl(1 - \tfrac{k_e}{E}\bigr)^{n}\Bigr).
\end{equation}
\end{lemme}

\begin{proof}
A given expert is not chosen by a token with probability $1 - k_e/E$; by independence, it is chosen by none of the $n$ tokens with probability $(1 - k_e/E)^n$. By linearity of expectation, the expected number of experts chosen by at least one token is $E$ times the complementary probability.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[When speculation pays for an MoE read from disk]\label{prop:spec-moe}
If the bytes coming from disk are dominated by routed experts, verifying $k$ drafted tokens reduces the bytes read per produced token if and only if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:spec-moe}
\mathcal E(\alpha, k) \;>\; \frac{U(k+1)}{k_e}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Without speculation, a token reads $k_e$ experts per layer. The verification pass covers $k+1$ positions (the prefix and the $k$ drafted tokens) and reads $U(k+1)$ experts per layer; it produces $\mathcal E(\alpha,k)$ tokens on average (\cref{thm:amortissement}). Bytes per produced token drop if and only if $U(k+1)/\mathcal E(\alpha,k) < k_e$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollaire}[Speculation never pays on independent fine-grained experts]\label{cor:jamais}
For $k = 1$, the condition becomes $1 + \alpha > 2 - k_e/E$, i.e.\ $\alpha > 1 - k_e/E$: $\alpha > 0.977$ for DeepSeek-V4-Flash ($k_e/E = 6/256$), $\alpha > 0.980$ for Qwen3.8-Flash-Next ($10/512$) \calc. In the fine-grained limit ($k_e(k+1) \ll E$), $U(k+1)/k_e \to k+1 > \mathcal E(\alpha,k)$ for every $\alpha < 1$.
\end{corollaire}

\begin{proof}
By \cref{lem:union}, $U(2)/k_e = 2 - k_e/E$ and $\mathcal E(\alpha,1) = 1 + \alpha$. In the limit, $(1 - k_e/E)^{k+1} \approx 1 - (k+1)k_e/E$, so $U(k+1)/k_e \to k+1$; yet $\mathcal E(\alpha,k) = \sum_{i=0}^k \alpha^i < k + 1$ as soon as $\alpha < 1$.
\end{proof}

\textbf{Stone's regime (batch of one, weights on disk) is exactly the one where Cascade~\cite{cascade2025} observes that speculation slows MoE models down.} Speculation therefore pays, for a fine-grained MoE read from disk, only if the real union is far below independence (neighbouring tokens share their experts), or if the expert set of the verification is bounded: MoE-Spec~\cite{moespec2026} (fixed budget per layer), AcceptMoE~\cite{acceptmoe2026} (eligibility restricted to experts already cached: $\times2.06$, $-0.27$ accuracy point), EcoSpec~\cite{ecospec2026} and EVICT~\cite{evict2026} (lossless), DraftExpert~\cite{draftexpert2026} \litt. The real union over draft tokens is published for no model the machine can read.

\paragraph{Predicting experts does not reduce bytes.} Mixtral-offloading, ProMoE, HOBBIT (\cref{sec:moe-systemes}) predict in order to prefetch: this hides the loading latency behind computation (\cref{prop:volume}). In the disk regime, where the disk works all the time, prefetching only yields the step from the sum to the maximum of \cref{thm:toit}. Only caching and lossy substitution reduce the bytes coming from disk.

\paragraph{What it takes for a zero to become an unread byte.} In VRAM: column-major weights and a kernel that loads only the live columns (TEAL's kernel requires it: \texttt{assert weight.stride(1) > 1}); no such kernel exists in llama.cpp, whose quantized types are row-major. On disk: the granularity is the page, and an FFN neuron of Qwen3.5-9B at 4.5 bits (three vectors of 4\,096) weighs about 6.9\,KiB \calc, two pages, far below the size at which this disk delivers its throughput (\cref{fig:disque}).

\subsection{The measurement: unions, window, hot neurons}

\paragraph{The instrument.} \texttt{llama\_numpy.py} is a numpy forward pass of a \enquote{llama} model read from the GGUF, written from scratch, with a hook on the input of every projection. Model: SmolLM2-135M-Instruct F16 (30 layers, SwiGLU). Control: the perplexity of the numpy pass must match \texttt{llama-perplexity} within 0.5\,\% (threshold declared before); it gives 22.6768 against 22.6739 over 16 windows (0.013\,\%, largest gap 0.18\,\%), and \textbf{the control fails when broken} (RoPE rotation by halves instead of adjacent pairs: 232.8 against 10.4) \mes.

\textbf{On a dense SwiGLU without retraining, activation sparsity and speculative verification do not compose} (\cref{tab:masque-commun}): either each token keeps its mask and the pass reads almost everything, or tokens share a mask and quality collapses. The control falls at exactly 100\,\% when the target is the draft: the tool does measure agreement (\cref{tab:alpha}). A draft of the same family, half the size, returns at most 4.3 tokens per pass as a chain; a quantized copy of the target itself agrees much better (91.9\,\% at about 4.8 bits), hence SubSpec's acceptance length. \textbf{The naive substitute collapses}: the 9B Q2\_K requantized without an importance matrix takes perplexity from 8.47 to 105 and agreement to 34\,\%. A substitute is made with the best available quantization, not the simplest.

\subsection{The resident substitute, transposed}

SubSpec keeps a 4-bit copy of the target in VRAM and streams the 16-bit target over the link from 128\,GB of RAM. At Stone's, 16\,GB of RAM impose an already quantized target (about 4.8 bits) and 8\,GB of VRAM a very low-rate substitute. With the measured agreements, a 6\,GB VRAM budget for the substitute and a Q4\_K\_M target in RAM, the study computes for a 14B target (9.1\,GB, 1.42\,s pass, link loaded at 6.38\,GB/s): 0.70 tokens/s without speculation, \textbf{1.75 tokens/s ($\times 2.5$)} with the best substitute that fits (IQ2\_XXS, 5.1\,GB, $\alpha = 0.66$, $k^\star = 7$) \calc. A 2-bit substitute keeping 85\,\% agreement would take the 14B to about 3.6 tokens/s. \textbf{This is the exact point where quantization and speculation multiply}: extreme quantization no longer only reads less, it manufactures the draft.

\section{What is missing in the world}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Speculation with weights read from the NVMe when RAM is smaller than the model.} SpecExec (140--192\,GB of RAM), SubSpec (128\,GB), Sequoia, TriForce read weights from RAM; on 16\,GB and a 2.82\,GB/s disk, it is an unmeasured factor of 10.
\item \textbf{Trees under Windows without CUDA}: llama.cpp verifies chains, capped at $1/(1-\alpha)$ (\cref{cor:borne-chaine}).
\item \textbf{The real expert-union curves of 2026 fine-grained MoE models over draft tokens}: all profitability depends on the gap to \cref{eq:union}.
\item \textbf{Splitting 8\,GB of VRAM between draft and target}: each GB given to the draft goes back to the link or the disk; nobody publishes the optimum.
\item \textbf{Tree verification for delta-rule hybrids} (48\,MiB of state per node).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Recommendations, for the arbiter.} The cheapest and most accurate draft is the target itself, quantized very low and resident, or its native MTP module; verify trees, not chains; for MoE models, verify only within the experts already in memory and measure the loss; do not invest in per-token activation sparsity for the disk regime on SwiGLU models.

\begin{faux}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The sparsity model is small} (135\,M); large models are sparser, and the correlation between neighbouring tokens may be stronger.
\item \textbf{The per-token top-$k$ mask is not exactly TEAL} (calibrated thresholds, variable density); it has the same mean density.
\item \textbf{The constant, independent $\alpha$ assumption} overestimates $\mathcal E$ for long chains.
\item \textbf{Independent routing} is pessimistic; if neighbouring tokens share many experts, \enquote{optimal $k = 0$} falls.
\item \textbf{$F = 20$\,Tflop/s} is not measured; $n^\star$ is an order of magnitude.
\item \textbf{The \enquote{same top token} is not exactly $\alpha$}: forced by a text, first position only, greedy.
\item \textbf{The substitute agreements come from the 4B}; a 14B quantizes better at equal bits.
\item \textbf{The machines were shared}: perplexities do not depend on it, timings do.
\end{itemize}
\end{faux}
